Registry notes (verbatim, quoted):

1) User Story

Generation got 1.84x more expensive across the cave series and that is now the reason the world takes minutes to fill in. Before we optimize anything I want to know whether we are paying more PER noise evaluation than Minecraft does, or simply doing more evaluations because our cave family is richer - because those two answers lead to completely different decisions about what to keep and what to cut.

2) Technical Details

THE QUESTION, STATED SO IT CAN BE ANSWERED: vanilla builds the final density on a 4x8x4-block cell and interpolates, and so do we (the cave lattice is `4x48x4` cells -> 8-block Y, read trilinearly off `FieldC` - `gen.cpp` ~630-641, AC-0344/AC-0359). So the SCHEME is not the difference. The candidates are (a) more lattice POINTS, (b) more OCTAVES per point, (c) more FIELDS, and (d) non-lattice work vanilla has no equivalent for (the per-block carve scan, the per-block aquifer search). The deliverable is a cost per unit of each, so "richer caves" can be priced against "cheaper noise".

WHERE THE TIME IS DOCUMENTED TO GO (per chunk, AC-0292's split, total 5,711 us before AC-0360's -392):
- field build **1,502 us**; scan **1,630 us**; fill **2,129 us**; drip **238 us**.
- Within the fill, the aquifer is the named term: AC-0291 measured the Voronoi 4-nearest search at **~1,600 us/chunk** (14.3% of the chunk's cells), with a `max_fluid` sky-block fast path that skips 84% of air blocks.
- The cave family is SIX fields built on the cave lattice: cheese (vn3, seed+301), spag/nood/gate (fbm3, +303/304/305), layer (vn3, +302), entrance (vn3, +306). `oct` is 2 by default and 3 for AC-0288's primary cave octave.
- So the untested part is the SPLIT WITHIN the 1,502 us field build - which field, how many octave evaluations, and what each one costs per point.

STEP 0 - per-field timing (the right instrument, and it is additive not ablative):
- Add per-field wall counters in `gen_timing()` beside the existing `g_t_aquifer_us` / `g_t_drip_us` pattern: one accumulator per cave-family `build_field_*` call site (cheese, spag, nood, gate, layer, entrance). Permanent instrumentation, cheap, no world change.
- An ABLATION (stubbing a field to a constant and re-running) is the cross-check, NOT the primary method: it scatters the terrain, so it is diagnostic-only and its numbers must be labelled as such. If used at all, run each stub in its own branch with the world discarded, and never let an ablated tree's other fields be quoted as evidence.
- Report per field: us/chunk, lattice points (7x49x7 = 2,401 for this lattice), octaves, and **us per point per octave**. That last figure is the whole point of the ticket - it is the directly comparable number.

STEP 1 - the comparison, stated as a ratio against vanilla's scheme:
- Vanilla's reference to compute, using the documented scheme (4x8x4 cell, 16x384x16 chunk -> 5x49x5 = 1,225 in-plane lattice points, per-octave interpolation of the final density, terrain/cheese/carvers derived from that one function): octave-evaluations per chunk and us per octave-evaluation on this box. Measure the second half directly rather than quoting it from the literature - write a micro-benchmark that calls the SAME `fbm3`/`vn3` primitives this codebase uses, so the comparison isolates scheme against scheme and not machine against machine.
- Output: **"we do N octave-evaluations per chunk against vanilla's M, at the same cost per evaluation"** - or, if the per-evaluation cost is also higher, the ratio and the reason (hash cost, interpolation count, `lerp_gd` chains). Either answer is a result; do not force a story.
- Also price the two stages vanilla has no equivalent for: the scan (1,630 us - us per solid cell scanned, and how many cells that is) and the aquifer (us per block, and the candidate count after the `max_fluid` gate). These are the "d" candidate and must be reported separately from the noise, because "our noise is expensive" and "we scan per block where vanilla does not" are different findings with different fixes.

STEP 2 - hand the decision over, do not make it:
- Where the numbers say a specific cost is avoidable WITHOUT changing output (loop-invariant hoisting, a redundant margin recomputation across the 7x7 field, a gate in the wrong place), record it as a candidate with its price - but do not implement it here.
- Where the cost buys fidelity (the cave family is deliberately five fields where vanilla has cheaper period-based equivalents; the layer field rides 4 samples/period at vn3), say so plainly and give the per-feature price. "Keep the caves, they cost X us/chunk" is a legitimate and useful result.

WHAT MUST NOT MOVE: the cave lattice resolution, the octave counts, the field count, and H. This ticket attributes cost; it does not tune. If any candidate fix would change generated output, it is a separate ticket needing the full genhash/genprobe/farab/ladder/thash gate set (H byte-exact, `h_mismatch` 0, the AC-0342 land-dry water contract). G0 zero SCRIPT ERROR, and the per-field counters must be proven not to move the world (genhash 25/25 on the instrumented tree).

RELATED: AC-0363 measures the regression's FRAME-time side and reads the existing `cols_full`/`cols_skip` lane split. This ticket measures the GENERATION-cost side and can run in parallel with it, but read AC-0363's step-0 result first - if it names a term outside the cave family or the aquifer, this ticket's scope narrows to that term.
