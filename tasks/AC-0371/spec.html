Registry notes (verbatim, quoted):

1) User Story: One search - the per-block aquifer lookup - costs more than a quarter of every chunk we generate and nearly seventy percent of the cave series' slowdown, and it is the one piece of our cave work that vanilla's scheme never interleaves. Cutting it is the largest single win available in generation.

2) Technical Details:
THE NUMBER (AC-0366, current tree, reconciled to the microsecond against AC-0363's independent measurement): the aquifer Voronoi search costs **113.8 ns x 14,057 searches = ~1,600 us per chunk**, i.e. 29.1% of the total 5,502 us/chunk and **67.7% of the 1.84x cave-series regression**. 84% of air blocks skip it, so the 14,057 searches are the ones that mattered; the whole non-lattice share of the chunk is 63%, and the other named per-block terms are the scan (1,604 us, 36-49 ns per scanned block) and drip (235 us, 2.4 ns/block).
WHAT IT DOES, so nobody optimises the wrong thing: AC-0291 gave every underground pocket its own water table via Bedrock's Voronoi aquifers - 16x12x16 cells with a 4-nearest lookup, and it is the feature the user asked for (perched lakes, lava pools behind stone rims, waterfalls). The ticket is to make the LOOKUP cheap, not to remove the feature.
WHERE THE TIME LIKELY GOES AND WHAT TO MEASURE FIRST: 113.8 ns per search is roughly 250-300 cycles, which for a 4-nearest-cell query over a small neighbourhood is expensive enough to suggest either an unbounded neighbourhood scan, redundant recomputation per block (the same cell lattice queried once per solid block rather than once per cell), or a cache-hostile access pattern. Measure WHICH before choosing a fix: count the candidate cells actually visited per search, check whether the same query repeats within a column (and whether a per-column or per-slab memo would collapse it), and price the alternatives (a coarser pre-pass that stamps a water-table id into the lattice and lets the per-block path read it; a spatial hash; a cheaper distance metric with the same nearest-cell outcome). AC-0366 also names the instrument gap: one-line accumulators for the search count and the scan count, which this ticket should add permanently if the arm keeps reporting them.
CONSTRAINTS: the aquifer's BEHAVIOUR must be preserved - the water tables, the rims, the lava rules and the AC-0342 land-dry / ocean-surface contracts are all measured and must be re-measured after the change (AC-0291's results page has the census: 17,983 land lake columns, 215 water/lava rims, 4,838 waterfall cells, land open water 0, ocean 1081/1081); H must not move (thash + farab h_mismatch 0); genhash will rebase ONLY if the water placement changes - if it does, that is a finding, not an expected rebase, because a pure lookup optimisation should be bit-identical; and the native lane rebuild/re-stage procedure in OPS.md is mandatory.
GATES: the aquifer census re-measured identical (or the difference explained and justified); the per-chunk cost measured with the same `GENMS` instrument AC-0366 used, against the 5,502 us/chunk baseline and the 1,600 us term; genhash 25/25 if behaviour is preserved; farab h_mismatch 0; ladder 20/20; meshprobe 1.0; halo green; boundary r4 in band; the battery green; and the R24 fill window re-measured with AC-0364's instrument so the win is stated in window seconds as well as microseconds (the window is bounded by the dispatch cost, so a cheaper chunk should SHORTEN it - that is the user-visible payoff).
CONTEXT: read tasks/AC-0366/AC-0366-results.html (the price list) and tasks/AC-0291/AC-0291-results.html (the feature and its census) first. DRAIN (AC-0369) and the random-tick port (AC-0370) are separate and address different shares; this is the generation-side lever.
