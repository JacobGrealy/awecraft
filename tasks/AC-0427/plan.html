AC-0427 — plan: the remaining holes + the shading discontinuity across chunk lines

1. The defect (established by the report + AC-0420/AC-0423)

After AC-0423 closed the continuous ~0.55 m see-through slit (the tangent sheets' diverging edge lines), the user reports two remaining defects, both along the chunk seam lines: (1) holes — "not entirely random ... along lines and fairly evenly spaced": intermittent see-through gaps + floating grass/dirt wedge profiles at places along every seam; (2) shading — the per-chunk vertex lighting changes across a boundary, up to a 0.922 luminance step at same-geometry corners (the AC-0420 measurement), drawing a visible line even where the geometry is solid.

The periodicity of (1) is the key clue: a systematic curvature shortfall would be continuous (already fixed); holes that repeat at regular intervals along a line indicate a per-cell rule that fails at specific cells — here, wherever the terrain steps in height across the seam.

2. Root causes

2a. The holes — the seam-V extended the top faces only

AC-0423's extension was guarded fcv == FCV[2] (single-quad paths) / fi == 2 (merged): top-face edge rows only. At a solid height step across a seam, the higher side's extended top-face sliver overhangs the lower side by the V width (~0.6 m at terrain height) — but the higher side's outward side face (the cliff) stayed on the exact seam plane. Between the sliver's overhanging bottom edge and the lower ground there is an open under-void: a floating wedge you can see through, exactly one per stepped cell — hence the periodic, evenly-spaced holes. The flat case (equal tops) self-hides; the step case does not. Measured live: 50 solid step cells across the 18 unique seams of the 3×3 spawn block, 0/50 with closed under-voids before the fix; the render shows the white see-through face + the wedge fronts.

2b. The shading — twin corners read different data

Modern lighting (AC-0398) computes each corner's (sl, oshade) from the 8 surrounding column tops + 4 normal columns in the chunk's own 18×18 snap grid. At a seam, the two twin corners sitting on the same world point read different inputs: the edge-side chunk reads its inward columns (its own real data) for the slots past the seam, while the halo-side chunk has no data there and falls back to a flat continuation of its own edge column. Whenever the inward column differs from the continuation (a 1-block step, a neighbour difference), the two twins get different occlusion → different (sl, oshade) → the luminance step along the line. Proof of the mechanism (worked example, the x16_z0 seam corner): every one of the 12 reads is enumerated for both twins — identical when the shared columns agree, and the single divergent read is exactly the off-grid/inward slot.

3. Fix design

3a. Holes — extend the edge column's outward side faces with the sliver

Remove the top-only guard in qwrite/qwrite_vc: bool seamv = (se != nullptr) (was fcv == FCV[2]). The seam_ext_corner hook already fires for any corner exactly on a chunk edge (integer sums, exact float equality) and computes the same per-cell E(t,i), so the edge column's outward side faces (the px==0/16 corners of a side face) now extend by the same amount as the top face. At a step, the extended cliff face's bottom edge sits at the lower ground's top-face level → the under-void is closed; at level cells the side face's extension is invisible (it meets the neighbour's face in the fold crease, the AC-0423 geometry). No switch: this is geometry correctness — completing the AC-0423 V closure, not a look change; and with sphere_r = 0 (the flat-world path, the arm ctxs, the face path) the tables are all zero and the mesh stays byte-identical to pre-AC-0423.

3b. Shading — the seamc clamp, behind seam_light

Add a seamc bool to the C++ ctx. In s_modern_corner, wrap the grid-read in a clamp: for a boundary corner (west gx == 1 / south gz == 1 in the read-center grid), any read inward of the seam (x > gx / z > gz) returns self — the corner's own edge-column top. Then both twins' 12 reads reduce to the same three shared sources: the own edge column (real data on one side, the ring copy on the other), the neighbour's edge column (the ring on one side, own data on the other), and the seam column itself — so with fresh rings the twins get identical (sl, oshade) → no step. Colour-only: no vertex moves, no geom_epoch bump; the toggle re-dispatches via the tex_refresh drain.
