1. The holes (defect 1) — root cause and census

Root cause: AC-0423's seam-V extension was top-faces-only (the fcv == FCV[2] guard). At a solid height step across a seam, the higher side's extended top-face sliver overhangs the lower side by the V width (~0.6 m) while its outward side face (the cliff) stayed on the exact seam plane — leaving an open under-void under every overhang: one floating wedge per stepped cell, evenly spaced along the line exactly where the terrain steps (the user's periodicity clue). Fix: qwrite/qwrite_vc now compute seamv = (se != nullptr) — the edge column's outward side faces extend with the sliver; at a step the extended cliff face's bottom edge lands on the lower ground and closes the void. No switch (geometry correctness; the sphere_r = 0 flat path stays byte-identical).

3. The shading (defect 2) — root cause and luminance step, before / after

Root cause: modern lighting (AC-0398) computes each corner's (sl, oshade) from the surrounding column tops in the chunk's own 18×18 snap grid. Twin corners on the same world point straddling a seam read different inputs: the edge-side chunk reads its inward columns (real data) past the seam; the halo-side chunk has no data there and falls back to a flat continuation of its own edge column. Any difference between those → different occlusion → the luminance step along the line (AC-0420 measured up to 0.922). Fix: the seamc ctx flag — in s_modern_corner, the inward reads of a boundary corner (west gx==1 / south gz==1) are evaluated as the corner's own column (self), so both twins use only SHARED data and get identical (sl, oshade). Colour-only (no geometry, no geom_epoch bump), behind seam_light (default ON; key-absent C++ = OFF = pre-fix byte path).

Measurement: per seam, per along-edge cell, the TWIN corners are paired (the highest-y top-face vertex of each side's seam band = the column-top corner; the first measurement averaged the whole band, which mixes in the AC-0423 sliver outer corners 0.66 m apart with legitimately different shading — a constant −658 mm gap proved the mixed pairing; the corrected pairing is used for BOTH columns of the table, so the comparison is like-for-like). Cells split into level twins (same height, |Δy|<0.5 — the true seam-artifact metric) and step twins (legitimate terrain-step shading: the higher ledge sees more open sky).

  • Level twins (the seam artifact): OFF leaves 2 residual same-height cells (x16_z16 cell 15 at 0.922; z16_x16 cell 8 at 0.102); ON leaves 190 of 191 at exactly 0.000. The z16_x16 cell is fixed by the clamp (0.102 → 0.000). *The x16_z16 cell 15 (world (15…16, z≈31)) is a one-block neighbour-ring data difference (a stale copy of the neighbour's edge column at one cell, far from spawn) — a data-freshness issue the read-clamp cannot fix (it makes the reads consistent, it does not refresh the data); it does not sit on the measured acceptance view and is logged as follow-up §6.
  • Step twins: the 0.2–0.92 steps are legitimate terrain-step shading (a 1-block step across the seam: the higher ledge sees open sky, the lower ledge sees the cliff face — different world points, different lighting, no seam defect). The ON column's consistency contract (both sides shaded from the shared data only) shifts some step cells' values vs OFF (x16_z0 0.400→0.804, z16_x16 0.804→0.922): OFF lets the edge-side chunk read its own inward column (locally accurate) while the halo side approximates — a per-chunk inconsistency; ON makes both sides use the same shared inputs. The visible "weird shading along the line" was the same-point discontinuity; that is 0.000 across the region.
