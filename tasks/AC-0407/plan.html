AC-0407 — plan: the clouds get brighter, a real skyline, and an interior you can be in

Spec source: tasks/TASKS.yaml AC-0407 notes (no spec.html exists in the ticket folder — the notes are the spec; recorded as a deviation). The user's three sentences, verbatim from the ticket: (1) the clouds are still too dark; (2) they have no variance in height so they read as a uniform layer at a single altitude; (3) passing through them does not feel like being inside anything.

Goal

Turn the three sentences into three measured changes on the same tree the user is flying:

  1. Brighter without flattening — lift the cloud's light by levers that are NOT the rejected ambient-weight raise (0.7 flattened the spread to 39.1): a brighter effective albedo (a brighter sky value for the ambient fill, a lift of the single-source air colour), a brighter multiple-scattering octave (deeper MS penetration into the shadowed interior), and a forward-scatter glow toward the sun. Target: the underside/interior reads bright against the sunlit-top reference, and the brightness spread stays above the no-cloud floor (~40.2).
  2. A real skyline (the structural fix — the strongest clue) — the annulus currently has a density field with no vertical profile, so every column fills 275–400 m uniformly: one slab at one altitude. Give the layer a flat base and lumpy tops: dense at the base, thinning toward a top whose altitude is modulated by the low-frequency weather (the tops vary in altitude by a visible amount). Also thicken the layer upward (400 → 450 m, base stays at 275 m, clear of the ~139 m ground) so the skyline has room. Target: a measured spread of cloud-top altitudes in metres where there was none (0).
  3. An interior you can be in — inside the annulus the view fills with structure at varying depth, brightens toward the light, and is genuinely occluding. The thickening + denser base adds depth/occlusion; the glow is the "brighten toward the light". Target: the inside-the-annulus frame shows a surrounding bright volume (coverage + luminance), not a thin grey film.
