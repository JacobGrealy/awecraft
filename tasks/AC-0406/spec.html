Registry notes (verbatim, quoted):

1) User Story: Clouds should be bright. Right now every cloud is dark, because they are lit only by direct sunlight and everything the sun does not hit directly goes black - which from underneath, where the player spends most of their time looking at clouds, is all of them.

2) Technical Details:
REPORTED BY THE USER 2026-10-07, one line: "why are all the clouds dark?" THE CAUSE IS KNOWN AND WAS REPRODUCED BY THE PREVIOUS RUN, which documented it and named it as the next lever rather than fixing it: the raymarched volume introduced by AC-0405 is lit by SELF-SHADOWING ALONE. Each sample marches toward the sun, and the result is multiplied by a PEAK-NORMALISED Henyey-Greenstein phase. There is NO AMBIENT OR SKY FILL and NO MULTIPLE SCATTERING. The consequence is that scattering is near zero for any sample not directly sunlit AND forward-scattering relative to the eye, so cloud undersides, interiors and shadowed faces - which is to say most of what a player sees from below - render black. AC-0405's own report records "interior/underside reads dark (no diffuse-fill term - named as the one-line next lever)". The coordinator reported that to the user as a limitation; the user's question is the correct response to it, and it should have been fixed in the same round.
WHAT TO ADD, in order of importance, all standard in this technique and all cheap relative to the raymarch that already exists:
(a) AN AMBIENT OR SKY FILL TERM. A cloud is lit from the whole sky dome, not only from the sun. THIS PROJECT ALREADY HAS THE COLOUR TO USE and it must be reused rather than invented: the single-source air colour that the fog, the dome haze and the sky horizon all share (AeroLib.fog_display, pushed as u_air / the environment fog colour). The standing rule that there is exactly one source for the air colour applies here - do NOT introduce a fourth colour for clouds.
(b) A MULTIPLE SCATTERING APPROXIMATION. Light that scatters inside a cloud does not stop at the first bounce; the standard cheap treatment is to evaluate the light march two or three times with DECREASING EXTINCTION for each successive octave and sum the contributions, which is what turns a black shadowed mass into a soft grey one. Guerrilla's Horizon Zero Dawn work - already the reference for this layer's lighting model - describes exactly this.
(c) BLEND THE PHASE TOWARD ISOTROPIC. A peak-normalised Henyey-Greenstein term is very directional by construction; mixing it partway toward isotropic (which is to say toward a constant) is what allows a cloud to be lit by the sky as well as by the sun without losing the silver lining when the sun is in front of the eye.
ACCEPTANCE IS THE USER'S VIEW MEASURED. The frames that matter are the ones looking UP at the clouds from the ground and from inside the annulus - AC-0405's r120-on.png and r300-on.png are the before set - and the measurement is the luminance of the UNDERSIDE and INTERIOR of a cloud mass against its sunlit top, before and after. A ratio near 1 is a flat cloud; the previous flat deck failed that way; the target is enough range to show form without the shadowed side going black. ALSO DO NOT LOSE WHAT AC-0405 GAINED: the brightness spread of 68.9 against the reference's 32.4 and the parallax between layers must survive, so measure the spread after the change as well and report both. Also confirm the cloud is no longer grey-neutral in tint but keeps the reference's saturated cast (181,229,190 at the disc centre).
COST: the raymarch currently runs at 781 s against 496 s for the feature off at the same resolution, about 1.5 to 2 FPS on the test box. Additional light octaves cost real time, so state the added cost and keep it inside the run budget; if two octaves cannot fit, one ambient term with a sensible falloff is better than three octaves that do not run.
