AC-0444 — the teal band in the sky above the ridge

Verdict: the band is not the sky — it is the lod_avg far LOD tier (the med/low average-colour placeholder), specifically its a=0 (unlit/side) faces, which are the AC-0443 “R24 horizon dark patch.” It is a far-LOD / tier-seam artifact, not a symptom of the five accumulated sky fixes. Those five fixes are correct and keep holding; none of them touches the far tier’s face shading, which is where the band actually comes from.

Where the band comes from

structural · far-LOD one-off · sky

The band is a horizontal strip of the distant placeholder terrain that curves up into the view above the local horizon. At the grazing angle the camera sees the vertical sides of the far terrain boxes — the a=0 faces (the heightmap “sky” channel is 0 at/below the terrain top, i.e. unlit). Those faces render at the cave-floor brightness lit = 0.2 + u_amb·day.r (u_amb = AMB_SKY_L = 0.35, the AC-0443 lift) multiplied by day.rgb (the sky display colour — a teal tint) and a ±6% jitter, so they read as a dark teal band far darker than the shaded flank of the same ridge in the near (chunk_lit) tier. That brightness gap across the tier boundary is the AC-0443 “R24 horizon dark patch,” which AC-0443 lifted partially (u_amb) but never fully closed.

It is fog-gated: the far tier is a distance placeholder built and rendered only while the engine environment fog is on (it fills the distance out to the fog wall, u_fog_far = 332 m). With fog off the band region is empty sky.

Is it a symptom of the five accumulated sky fixes? — No

The band is not the sky pass. At the band’s elevation the aero sky shader (live uniforms w=0, ws=0, u_horizon=1, u_no_floor=1, T=0.5) computes a bright gradient (208,240,250) at (1200,342); the band renders (41,62,69). No value of the space weights w/ws makes the sky pass dark there, and the aero-off path (sky_off, default colours) also renders bright. The band is opaque terrain drawn in the sky region.

Consolidation recommendation (the valid outcome the ticket allows)

Do not consolidate the sky model for this band — there is no sky defect to consolidate. The honest answer: the band is a far-LOD / tier-seam artifact (the a=0 faces of the lod_avg placeholder), pre-existing and cloud-independent, and it is the AC-0443 “R24 horizon dark patch.” The fix belongs to a far-LOD ticket, not this sky ticket, and should not be a suppression patch. Two legitimate shapes:

  • Close the tier seam properly: shade the far tier’s a=0 faces to match the chunk_lit shaded brightness at the same ridge flank (the u_amb lift is a partial parity — raise/retarget it so an a=0 far face and an a=0 near face of the same slope read the same). This removes the band at its source for every grazing-angle ridge, not just this one.
  • Or accept it as a known far-LOD limit and document it as the standing R24 dark patch, so a future far-LOD pass (real far terrain, or a seam-blend between the tier and the body’s haz_d rim) owns it — rather than re-patching the sky, which does not cause it.

Either way: the five sky fixes stay as they are; the sky model is not the accumulated-conditions liability the ticket fears, for this feature.

Harness observations (harvest candidates)

  • Node.find_children() returns empty for every pattern/type (even “*” / “”) on a node with ~1468 children on this build — hand-walk get_children() recursively.
  • ArrayMesh.aabb is read-only; ArrayMesh has no get_surface_material (guard with has_method); Basis has no .columns (use cb * Vector3.UP); is_ray_intersect is unreliable on degenerate-aabb and the satellite body mesh — treat a manual raycast as corroborating and the A/B isolations as ground truth.
  • Under llvmpipe hitches _physics_process runs ~6 physics steps per engine frame (max-physics-steps catch-up): a drain counted in physics_frame awaits is only ~500 engine frames. There is no freeze and no node replacement — a process-phase probe simply never reached its next print before the run ended.
  • The sky is Environment.BG_SKY with a custom sky_material; with aero off the shader runs its defaults (u_sky_alt=0 → the sky_off world-Y path), so an aero-off A/B still renders a (different) sky, not a flat background.
