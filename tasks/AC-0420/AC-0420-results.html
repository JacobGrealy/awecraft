Item 2 — “The player slides on the chunks without pressing any buttons”

“The player slides on the chunks without pressing any buttons, I assume this is gravity related”

Reproduced and fixed. A temporary probe arm (removed at closeout) dropped a frozen idle player at five spots and integrated the real physics for 20 s each.

Per-frame mechanism (reconstructed from the live velocity trace): every frame the slide removal is (v·n̂)n̂ with n̂ the exact radial (0.0000° off — the up-frame is fresh and exact); the collision surface under the player is the rigid per-column facet (up to 0.81° off local vertical on 1-block steps) plus the per-cell 1 m collision triangles, which are down-wound (n.y<0) and double-sided. On stepped terrain gravity’s tangential component (G·sinθ) bleeds through the step contacts faster than the walk lerp (k = 12/s) can cancel it: steady state v = G·sinθ/k ≈ 27 mm/s, exactly the measured rate, exactly downhill. On a large flat pad (no step side-contacts) the bleed is 0 — exactly the measured 0. The player loop has no friction and no post-slide velocity handling, so the residual velocity persists forever.

Fix

# godot/player/player.gd — after move_and_slide():
if is_on_floor() and not flying and not in_water and not in_lava:
    if absf(tx) < 0.001 and absf(tz) < 0.001:          # no movement input
        var vt := Vector2(vloc.x, vloc.z).length()
        if vt > 0.0 and vt < 0.2:                        # idle threshold
            var vw := b.transposed() * velocity          # POST-slide velocity
            vw.x = 0.0
            vw.z = 0.0
            velocity = b * vw

Grounded + no input + below 0.2 m/s: zero the tangential part of the post-slide velocity (rebuilding from the pre-slide vloc would re-inject the gravity impulse — caught and fixed in a first revision). Walking (4.3 m/s), jumps, flight and water are untouched. Not an architectural change (no new autoload/subsystem; local physics-tuning guard).

What the fix is (options for the coordinator)

  • A — lighting continuity at seams (recommended): make the per-vertex light bake agree across chunk edges (shared light samples on boundary vertices / a seam blend in the last meter). This removes the 0.5 shade step, the dominant visible line up close. Architectural (lighting pipeline) — its own ticket.
  • B — depth: raise the near plane and/or a polygon offset on the seam band. Kills the long-range z-fight flicker of the 1.6 mm overlap. Near-plane change ripples to all close views — measure first.
  • C — cosmetic: a 1-pixel dark grout line is legitimate voxel aesthetic (Minecraft has block AO lines); keep the overlap, kill only the flicker (B) and the shade step (A).

No fix applied in this ticket — the measurements above are the decision input (the ticket says: state the numbers, let the numbers pick the fix). Nothing in item 1’s symptoms is a data or streaming defect: geometry is closed (−1.6 mm overlap, 0.01 mm V).

Item 3 — “high lods disappear much sooner than medium and low as you fly to orbit”

“high lods disappear much sooner than medium and low as you fly to orbit, why?”

Answer: all tiers share one hard 332 m discard wall (u_fog_far), so the FARTHEST (coarsest) tier is the first to cross it as altitude rises — and a real bug (the AC-0401 push gap, below) could additionally pin that wall to the shader default 332 m for any config where the live fog wall is not 332 m. Read the table from the bottom: the coarsest band (C) is already 90 % fogged at only 81.6 m altitude and is gone at 181.4 m; band B dies at 296.6; A at 321.4; the real terrain survives to 332. So “high LOD” (= LOD level 2/3, the coarse distant bands) disappears much sooner than medium/low — by construction of the single shared wall, and the user’s premise holds exactly. (If “high LOD” meant “high-detail”, the data says the opposite: the near high-detail tiers are the last to go.) The band-C outer ring (272–400 m) is never drawn at any altitude — at ground level it is already outside the 332 m wall.

The bug found and fixed: the AC-0401 push gap for late-built materials

world.gd _ac0401_push() pushed the live fog wall (u_fog_far) to the chunk materials only when the wall value changed. The chunk ShaderMaterials are created lazily on first mesh build — after the first push had already run against an empty material cache — so they were never updated and kept the shader default 332.0 for the life of the session. At the user’s R24/F83 that default happens to equal the live wall (332), so the bug was invisible in this exact config; at any other render distance the terrain would die off at 332 m regardless of the fog window (e.g. R50 → live wall 714, materials stuck at 332 → the whole ring 332–714 missing, “terrain disappears at a fixed distance”).
