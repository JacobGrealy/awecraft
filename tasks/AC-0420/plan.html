AC-0420 — plan: measure first, then fix

The user's three reports, in their words: "visible cracks between all of the chunks" / "the player slides without pressing any buttons, I assume gravity" / "high lods disappear much sooner than medium and low as you fly to orbit, why?". Two of the three were answered before with work on neighbouring mechanisms (AC-0397 band-A fill, AC-0416 far-tier placement — both measured to zero and therefore NOT these), so every item gets a number before any fix.

1. Goal

  1. CRACKS — measure a high-tier chunk boundary directly: vertex positions on both sides of the shared edge (across-seam gap/overlap), the along-seam V depth, the vertex normals and the baked vertex-colour (light / AO / face-shade channels) across it. Classify the crack: geometry, shading, or depth. Close render(s) of the boundary. The known geometry facts (to be re-measured, not re-derived): AC-0362 grout = 1.6 mm in-plane overlap at every seam; the folded-net residual is "almost entirely along-seam/radial" (V-crack class, up to ~5.4 cm in band); depth precision at near=0.1 / far=4000 is ≈ 0.06 mm @10 m, 6 mm @100 m, 24 mm @200 m, 95 mm @400 m.
  2. SLIDE — idle drift on a flat pad (away from the column centre) and on the natural spawn slope: rate (m/s), direction vs the tangent plane and vs the column-centre radial, terrain. Hypothesis to test (the user's own): the radial gravity −_up·26 is applied to the velocity every physics frame while grounded; the contact normal (the per-column FACET, up to d/R ≈ 0.002 rad off the exact radial) cancels only its normal part, leaving a tangential component g_t = 26·d/R (d = distance from the column centre, up to ~8–11 m) that the ground lerp (k=12/s) damps to a steady v_ss = g_t/12 ≈ 4–6 mm/s toward the column centre. Predicted: measurable drift on flat ground, direction ≈ toward the column centre; zero in the air.
  3. TIER ORDER — the direct answer: a per-tier altitude table. Runtime facts to measure: the live on EVERY near material (the 4 chunk_lit_*.gdshader kinds in ChunkScript._mat_cache, the two fluid_anim_*, and lod_avg pushed by main.gd from env.fog_depth_end) — the AC-0401 claim "all six discard at one distance" was verified on shader TEXT; the runtime uniform values (including the change-gated push and the 332.0 shader default) are unverified. Plus the user-config tier extents (sim 4 / med 8 / low auto-14 / render 24, fog 83 % → wall 332 m, fog window [100, 332], camera far 4000/9000). Then, per tier: the altitude at which it stops being drawn (strict, its inner edge crossing the 332 m view-distance sphere) and the altitude at which it is ≥90 % fogged (visual). If a tier has a second, earlier limit unconnected to the shared wall, state it plainly — that is the answer.

4. The three measurements (and what they must show)

  1. Seam census (arm, headless, the two spawn seams (0,0)|(1,0) and (0,0)|(0,1), +2 further-out seams): across-seam overlap/gap per matched boundary vertex (mm, expect ≈ −1.6 mm overlap ± float32 transform rounding), along-seam V depth (mm–cm), normal angle across (deg), per-channel vertex-colour Δ (expect ~0 if the light/AO field is continuous — a non-zero Δ is the shading-crack finding). Plus the depth-precision table. Then the renders decide: a line in the mesh (visible at 10 m where precision is sub-mm), a colour step (shading), or a mottle only at mid range (depth).
  2. Idle drift (arm): flat pad, player offset ~8.5 m from the (0,0) column centre, no input, 20 s: total drift, rate, direction vs the column-centre radial (predicted g_t = 26·d/R, v_ss = g_t/12 ≈ 4.5 mm/s toward (8,8)); repeat on the natural spawn slope (drift should gain the downhill component). Post-fix: ≈0 on both, walking/jump/spherewalk unchanged.
  3. Tier table (arm): per tier {0, A, B, C}: inner/outer extent (m, from the live knobs), the runtime actually on its materials, the strict stop-altitude sqrt(wall² − inner²), the visual 90 %-fog altitude. User config: wall 332, window [100, 332]; predicted strict table ≈ C 229 / B 299 / A 322 / T0 332 m — i.e. the COARSE tiers stop first, the shared wall holds; if that is what the numbers say, the "finest vanishes first" perception is the tier EXTENT (128 m vs 368 m) plus the fog window, and the answer is that statement with the table — no manufactured fix. A material found at the 332.0 default while the wall is elsewhere (or a tier missing where its extent says it should draw) is the defect to fix.
