AC-0480 — the far tier does not look like the near tier, and it should

Coordinator contract. Medium builder applies it with tasks/templates/implement.md. Classify the seam with measurements before fixing, and check the shared-cause hypothesis first because it could make this one fix instead of three.

1. The report and the spec

User, with screenshots: "There are seams in the high LOD terrain outside of the sim distance, why, shouldn't high and sim look exactly the same?"

The user's expectation IS the spec: the high-LOD tier is the same world at lower detail. Any visible boundary between the simulated near field and the far field is a defect by definition. What may change with distance is detail — never content, colour, lighting or height.

2. Check the shared cause FIRST, before any tier-specific work

In the same message the user reported three seams in three tiers: this one (high-LOD terrain at the sim boundary), a seam through the clouds and sky (AC-0481), and a seam across the satellite disc (AC-0438, now confirmed real). Three straight seams in three unrelated subsystems is worth one look for a common tiling / wrap / projection convention before three separate fixes. Spend a bounded effort on this first and report what you find either way — even "they are unrelated" is worth knowing, and it is cheap to check whether the seams share an axis or a world-space position.

3. Classify the seam (the ticket's core task)

Four candidates, and the capture cannot distinguish them — measure:

  1. (a) GEOMETRY discontinuity — different heights, or a gap/overlap where the near mesh ends and the far mesh begins.
  2. (b) LIGHTING discontinuity — one side lit, the other not; different ambient or normal treatment.
  3. (c) TEXTURE/COLOUR discontinuity — different palettes, or a filtering seam where near blocks meet the far tier's averaged/baked texture.
  4. (d) OVERLAP / Z-FIGHT — both tiers drawing the same ground.

How to tell them apart: sample a line of pixels across the boundary and report the step size and its character. A hard 1-pixel step is geometry or overlap; a soft gradient over several pixels is colour/averaging; a step present in a depth/normal sense but not in colour is lighting. Report the numbers. The same discipline measured AC-0438 and AC-0402 correctly and it is expected here.

4. Then find the mechanism

Where the tiers meet is decided by the resident window and the residency predicate (AC-0469 stage A/B) and the far tier's mesh floor (world.gd:8029, "the far-tier mesh floor"). The far tier is charted and baked (AC-0469 stage D, re-shipped AC-0478) and the handover rule says the near field draws until the body actually covers it (AC-0451). A seam is two of those disagreeing about the same ground. Establish specifically:

  • Is the far tier's height for a cell computed from the same data the near mesh builds from? If it is an average over a cell, a height offset at the boundary is inherent to the averaging and the fix is different (a blend/handover) from a wrong-sample bug.
  • Is the far tier's colour the average of the same blocks the near tier shows, or a different palette/bake?
  • Does the handover (AC-0451) actually cover the gap at the moment the seam is visible, or does the seam appear where coverage has not yet happened (the AC-0451 measurement found the aerial see-through was mostly UNBUILT CHUNKS, not the handover — the same distinction applies here)?

4a. REPAIR (round 109) — the fix already exists and is DISABLED

The builder established the classification and then correctly returned PLAN-INSUFFICIENT rather than editing outside its fence. What it found is the whole ticket:

  • The seam is (b) LIGHTING-dominated with a (c) residual colour term. lod_avg.gdshader is ENGINE-SHADED ON TOP OF ITS OWN LIGHT (double shading), while the near tier is unshaded with L = 0.20 + 0.80*m_direct. Heights are the same data by construction (h_mismatch 0), the chunk sets are disjoint (so not overlap), and the band IS built where the user sees it (so not coverage). The residual colour term is coarse per-cell palette sampling (hue brown→beige).
  • A TESTED PARITY FIX ALREADY EXISTS: lod_avg_seam.gdshader, built at AC-0446 with an AFTER profile of −3…−15 and "no dark step" — and it is behind the far_seam switch, DEFAULT OFF.
  • Measured step it removes: the 1→2 tier boundary sides read (50,40,31) vs (135,116,103) = 2.5–3x; the 0→1 sim edge measures 0 step on open terrain, so the sim boundary is already clean and the defect is specifically the averaged band's inner edge.

SO THE TASK IS: ENABLE IT, AND PROVE IT WITH THE SAME PIXEL MEASUREMENTS. The switch's default lives at godot/autoload/settings.gd:209, which is why this plan had no anchor for it. Flip far_seam to default ON, then re-measure the 1→2 boundary sides and the AC-0446 ratio, and compare against the recorded (50,40,31) vs (135,116,103) and the AC-0446 −3…−15 profile. Do NOT build a new shader — the point of this ticket is that the fix was already written, tested and left off.

If flipping the default is judged wrong (e.g. it must stay opt-in for a reason recorded in settings.gd or the AC-0446 notes), say what that reason is and make the ON path the default anyway if the measurement supports it — the user is reporting the OFF path's appearance as a defect, so "it is optional" is not an answer to the report.
