AC-0480 — seams BETWEEN chunks inside the high-LOD tier

Coordinator contract, rewritten (round 116) after the user rejected the first close. Medium builder applies it with tasks/templates/implement.md. The leading mechanism is already located in the code; measure it, then fix it at the source.

1. Why this is open again — my first close was wrong

I closed this on a measurement of the 1→2 tier boundary (the averaged band's inner edge) and reported the dark step and grey tint gone. The user's correction, verbatim:

"AC-0480 did not fix the seam issue, the seam issue is in high lod between chunks within the high lod tier, not just the boundary between high lod and sim lod. If high and sim lod are using different shaders they should not be."

2. The shader question, ANSWERED from the code (verify, then build on it)

The two tiers do NOT share a shader, and the difference is structural rather than a setting:

  • NEAR/SIM TIER: StandardMaterial3D with the block texture atlas (chunk.gd _opaque_material(at) ~:1576, _cutout_material ~:1591) — UV-mapped, per-block textures.
  • FAR/HIGH-LOD TIER: a ShaderMaterial running core/lod_avg.gdshader (world.gd _lod_avg_mat() ~:1609) — NO UVs, and the vertex colour is a QUANTIZED AVERAGE FACE COLOUR per sample, with a noise term to break up the flat faces.

So "they should not use different shaders" is right in spirit and impossible in letter: a far tier at that distance cannot afford per-block textured geometry. The two representations are necessarily different — textured voxels versus averaged vertex colours. What MUST match is the resulting APPEARANCE, and the ticket's job is to make that true, not to merge two irreconcilable representations.

3. THE LEADING MECHANISM FOR THE INTRA-TIER GRID — measure this first

From world.gd's own description of the far tier (b~:1500-1510, read it): each sample's per-face colour is the average of cached per-block-face colours over its non-air blocks, and the greedy merge key is the QUANTIZED FACE COLOUR plus the outermost plane. QUANTIZATION IS PER-SAMPLE AND PER-CHUNK. Therefore two adjacent chunks can quantize what is effectively the same terrain to different colour buckets, and the greedy merge cannot join across the chunk boundary — producing a visible seam exactly at every chunk edge, which is the plaid grid the user photographed.

THE TEST THAT DECIDES IT: if the seams sit on the CHUNK GRID (16-cell spacing, i.e. a regular lattice matching the chunk size) it is the quantization/merge mechanism. If they sit on some other interval, it is not. Measure the seam spacing and compare it to the chunk spacing — that single number decides the whole ticket. Then, if confirmed, check whether the quantization bucket boundaries or the per-chunk merge are the culprit: a global (chunk-independent) quantization would let adjacent chunks land in the same bucket, whereas a per-chunk-relative one guarantees mismatches.

3a. ✅ REPAIR (round 116) — MY QUANTIZATION THEORY WAS REFUTED; THE REAL CAUSE IS FOUND AND THE FIX IS AUTHORISED

A medium run measured the deciding number and overturned §3. Its findings stand:

  • THE LATTICE IS NOT THE CHUNK GRID. The far tier's seam lattice is its own 2 m averaging cell (the 8×8×8/slab band) — 1/8 of the 16 m chunk, 8× denser. So §4c applies: the quantization/merge theory is refuted, and "between chunks" in the user's report is a PERCEPTION — the lattice crosses every eighth line, which reads as chunk-spaced.
  • THE QUANTIZATION IS ALREADY CHUNK-INDEPENDENT: q = floor(c * 31) is a pure function of colour (mesh.cpp:2869) and the emitted colour is the UNQUANTIZED origin-cell average (:2924-2926). The only chunk-relative far-tier term is the water UV origin, which affects water only. A chunk-edge seam is therefore structurally impossible — my hypothesis could not have been right.
  • THE NOISE HAS NO PER-CHUNK PHASE: it is 1.5 m world-anchored ±6% (lod_avg_seam.gdshader:112-114).
  • THE ACTUAL CAUSE: the near tier is per-block UV atlas plus per-vertex 256-level light, while the far tier is flat colour per 2 m cell plus a PER-CELL BINARY HALO SKY — a ~4× brightness step at every lit/unlit cell edge (world.gd:2810-2916). The seam IS the far tier's per-cell representation hard-stepping at every cell boundary. That is not a look preference, it is a binary term producing a 4× discontinuity, and it is the thing to fix.

AUTHORISED FIX — OPTION A, because it addresses the identified cause rather than masking it: (1) 3×3 cell smoothing in the C++ far-tier emit (avg_grid_emit) so cell boundaries do not hard-step, and (2) give the halo sky 3 LEVELS instead of a binary lit/unlit split, so a ~4× step becomes a graded transition.

Option B (coarsening the merge bucket from 1/31 to 1/15) is REJECTED as the primary fix: it changes colour precision to hide a brightness discontinuity rather than removing it. Recorded as rejected with that reason.

THIS FIX IS IN THE C++ LANE, SO A REBUILD IS REQUIRED AND MUST BE STATED AND RUN — gdext/src/mesh.cpp low_emit_avg/avg_grid_emit is the ONLY emit the game runs; the GDScript twin is harness-only, so measuring the twin proves nothing about the product. Also note the run flagged that band B did not materialise on this llvmpipe proxy (reproduced 3×, zero far-tier pixels), so the verification may need a vantage that actually draws band B — find one, or say plainly that the fix cannot be visually confirmed here and what the user should check.
