Verdict on the primitive: fundamentally wrong for this ask. The clouds are three flat, finite, player-following sheets. They are not a shell, a dome, or a screen effect over the disc — they are local weather planes that happen to exist at orbital altitude as well. The topmost sheet's footprint (the spherical region under its 6144²-block plane) covers 48.9% of the globe's area (measured, §4) — so the geometry alone is not the whole story: the sheet's own alpha is what kills it. Within that footprint only 3.0% of the surface has day alpha > 0.2 (top layer alone: 0.86% over its full area; mean alpha 0.015). And the sheet is a PLANE pinned near the north pole: from nadir views it occupies a shrinking fraction of the disc (96.9% at 15 km, 86.1% at 30 km) because the observer's line of sight to the disc's far side no longer intersects that fixed-height square, while the disc itself is a 4.1 km-radius spherical cap. That is exactly the user's screenshot: a smooth pale ball (AeroWash screen wash + the AC-0384 satellite body) with a scattering of small white puffs. A density tweak of a finite flat sheet cannot make it wrap the planet — the fix is the primitive.

2. The three-layer composite — who owns what

At altitude the planet's appearance is a composite of (per AC-0384/AC-0382/AC-0145 contracts, verified against satellite_body.gdshader, satellite_body.gd L395–434, aero.gd, harness.gd L26217):

LayerGeometry / spaceOwns
AeroWash (aero_wash.gdshader)screen-space additive quad 0.12 m in front of the camera, viewport-sizedthe ambient pale tint over the whole view (the "pale ball" tint). No geometry, no depth, no altitude dependence.
Satellite body (satellite_body.gdshader)12-face chart at radius R + SEA = 4126, translation-only nodethe textured whole-body. Opacity op = smoothstep(fog_far, render_edge, d) per fragment on TRUE world distance (AC-0384); hidden below the fog wall; limb haze haz = clamp((1−cosv)² + (1−smoothstep(edge, 4·edge, d)), 0, 1) mixed toward u_air; day/night via the AC-0382 sun term. No-pop argument: fixed 0.25 texel-to-chunk ratio (4 m texel vs 16 m chunk).
Flight band (AC-0145)scalar smoothstep(500, 2000, |pos−C| − R)the 6-DOF band driver; the natural fade driver for anything altitude-dependent.

Cloud shell (this change) — what it owns and does not own:

  • Owns: weather. Three concentric transparent shells at radii R + 275 / 330 / 400 (the same altitudes the quads hovered at, above sea level), world-anchored 3-D noise, sun-lit by the same DayNight.sun_direction convention AC-0382 wired into the satellite body (u_sun = −DayNight.sun_direction(t), u_day = 0.18 + 0.82·day, u_day_gain = 0.82·day). Its alpha is a function of world direction and time-of-day only — never of player distance.
  • No pop at the body's emergence: the body emerges per-fragment against the fog wall with the 0.25 ratio; the shell is one persistent object with no per-chunk or per-distance dissolve, so it contributes no new transition. As the player climbs through the flight band the shell's alpha under the eye is continuous (the shell is simply there at every altitude, exactly as the quads were at low altitude).
  • No double-counted limb haze: the shell's alpha carries a grazing fade smoothstep(0.02, 0.18, dot(n, V)) — the cloud mass thins to the silhouette exactly where the body's own limb haze is strongest, so the rim reads as one thing: body + its haze + a thinning cloud edge, not two overlapping white rims. (Draw order between the two transparent objects is engine-sorted; if the body's haze blends over the clouds that is the physically correct order — atmosphere is between the camera and the cloud deck.)
  • Does not own: the pale tint (AeroWash, unchanged), the body's texture/dissolve/haze (AC-0310/0384/0382, untouched), the flight band (AC-0145, untouched), fog (the shell stays fog_disabled, as the quads were — depth fog is tuned for the local 238–714 m band and would erase a 4300 m shell).

3. Design decision — make it a shell, keep everything else

Lowest complexity that achieves "wraps the planet": replace the three quads with three SphereMesh shells (unit sphere scaled to R + h, centered at (0, −R, 0)), and re-express the same pattern in 3-D value noise sampled on the unit direction normalize(worldpos − C). Everything else is reused, not reinvented: the same fbm structure (3 frequencies, 5 octaves), the same per-layer feature sizes in blocks (u_scale3 = 2π(R+h)/feature — a 256-block lobe stays a 256-block lobe, now everywhere on the globe), the same wind speeds (converted to rotation about world Y: ω = |wind|/(R+h) rad/s — east–west drift, polar stagnation for free), the same u_coverage/u_cloud_tint/night-darkening pipeline in _update_sky, the same AWECRAFT_CLOUD_T0 seed, the same AWECRAFT_CLOUDS flag. The only genuinely new things: 3-D noise in the shader, one coverage-window retune (the 0.62–0.88 window is what made the puffs sparse — see §4), the grazing fade, and sun-lambert lighting on the ALBEDO (not the alpha).

Weather is not generation: the pattern is pure runtime shader noise, no generator involvement, no seed in the world data — genhash must be byte-identical and is re-verified (§6).

4. Evidence — coverage before/after (numeric, no renders)

Method: a faithful re-implementation of the shader noise in numpy (.scratch/AC-0385/coverage.py) — the old 2-D ahash/anv/fbm2 verbatim, and the new 3-D ahash3/anv3/fbm3 with the same 5-octave structure — sampled on a 900×1440 equirect grid (area-weighted by cos lat) for the globe, and on a 700×700 nadir ray grid for disc coverage. Day = cloud_amount 0.75; night 0.30. "Coverage" is reported as the fraction of area with alpha > 0.2 (visible cloud), >0.05 (any cloud), and >0.5 (dense), plus the mean alpha — the distribution, not a single average.

4.3 Altitude, thickness, alpha

  • Altitude: shells at R+275 / R+330 / R+400 — the same radial altitudes (275/330/400 m above the surface plane) the old quads hovered at, so low-altitude cloud height is unchanged (~260 m above the ground for the top layer).
  • Thickness: the deck spans R+275…R+400 (125 m of radial extent, three concentric transparent shells), same as the old three quads at 275/330/400.
  • Alpha: per-fragment α = coverage-noise × per-layer cov (1.0/0.75/0.55) × cloud_amount (0.30 night … 0.75 day) × grazing fade. Peak day α ≈ 0.75 (top layer, dense lobe, facing the sun); mean disc α ≈ 0.37. The sun term scales the ALBEDO only (0.18 night floor … 1.0 noon, the satellite's AC-0382 set), never the alpha, so the weather pattern's coverage is time-of-day-independent for a given cloud_amount.

7. Deviations, caveats, and what the coordinator owes

  • One render is not available on this box (llvmpipe degraded), per the ticket — so the evidence is the coverage numbers + the three data maps in §4, not a screenshot. The forward_plus look of the shell (sun shading, grazing-fade softness, blend order vs the body) is verified only by construction; a coordinator render at orbital altitude is the natural next step.
  • Draw order between the cloud shell and the satellite body is engine-sorted (both are transparent, same center). If the body's haze blends over the clouds that is the physically correct order (atmosphere is between camera and deck); if the clouds blend over the body's haze the result is a slightly brighter limb. Either is acceptable; neither is a pop. Flagged so a coordinator render can confirm which order the engine picks.
  • Coverage is a tunable, not a constant. The 0.50–0.58 window gives 67.8% (day, α>0.2). If the user finds the deck too dense or too sparse, the window edges are the single knob (lower both for denser). I chose 68% as "broad and even, reads as weather, still has gaps" — but this is the one number most likely to be tuned by eye, which this box cannot do.
  • The genhash reference in HARNESS.md is stale. The row cites the AC-0367 reference, but AC-0387's coarse-LOD carve (a pre-existing, already-closed commit) moved the reference; my run matches the AC-0387b close-out set, not the AC-0367 one. Not caused by this change (pristine ≡ post-change), but the coordinator may want the genhash row re-pointed at the post-carve set.
  • No code was left behind: the temporary placement probe and the two shader-introspection scripts were removed; final git status shows only the four intended files (cloud shader, main.gd, aero.gd comment, ARCHITECTURE.md) plus the pre-existing TASKS.yaml status flip.
