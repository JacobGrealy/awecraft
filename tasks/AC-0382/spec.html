Registry notes (verbatim, quoted):

1) User Story: The planet looks round from orbit now, but the first view of a new world can hitch while its surface image is prepared, and the day/night line never moves across it - the body is lit as if the sun stood still.

2) Technical Details:
REMAINDER OF AC-0310, measured by its piece 2 and deliberately left for this ticket (2026-09-30): the whole-body satellite tier works (12 per-face 1024-square textures, a per-fragment dissolve, and a FIXED 0.25 texel-to-chunk ratio at every altitude, so the transition cannot pop in the resolution sense), but two things are owed and one is a no-op worth recording.
(a) OFF-THREAD OR SLICED BAKING: a non-canonical seed bakes at runtime, currently sliced on the main thread at 6 ms/frame, and the measured cost of that path is step mean 9.54 ms with a max of 171 ms (a PNG-deflate spike) against a full-frame max of 229 ms under R50 headless streaming. The shipped seed path is 360 ms wall. Far-generation was measured in piece 1 at 17.89 s over 196,196 chunks (the ticket's own 4.5 s estimate assumed 49,152 chunks - the home pair's 1 m columns need 19x that count), so the bake cannot be made fast, only made not-to-hitch. Move it off the main thread: the thread-generation and thread-mesh lanes already exist and the thread-gen pool measured 1-2 inflight against 545/s capacity during streaming work, so there is room; the builder notes this needs a C++ task type rather than a GDScript tweak. Measure the main-thread stall before and after, since the hitch is the failure mode, not the wall time.
(b) SUN-TRACKING DAY/NIGHT: the bake carries a fixed-sun lambert and the body is unlit, so brightness never changes and the terminator never moves across the planet. Make the shading follow the world's actual sun direction (the day/night system exists - check how the existing `daynight` arm and the world's sun drive the flat world's lighting, and reuse that convention rather than inventing a second one). This changes the bake's contract: either re-bake per sun position (expensive), bake the slope/normal channel and light it in the shader (cheap, and the shader already exists), or find a third way - say which and why.
(c) RECORD, DO NOT IMPLEMENT: a sub-pixel disc cull at altitude is a visual no-op because the disc is fully fogged above the band - so write that down where the next reader will find it rather than "fixing" it.
CONSTRAINTS: genhash 25/25 byte-identical (a render tier must not touch generation); the satellite arm stays green at both r4 and R50 with its ladder (hidden / rim / dissolve at nadir_op 0.5 / full) and the 0.25 ratio invariant intact; the standing set green (G0 zero SCRIPT ERROR, farab h_mismatch 0, ladder 20/20, meshprobe 1.0, halo ok, lightstate 0/0, r16 all sub-flags with far_holes 0/0 both bands, boundary r4 in band, the battery); and the piece-1 texture guard (two-axis variance on the written PNG bytes, negative-tested) must keep passing, because it exists precisely because a smear shipped once.
SAFETY: `export HOME=/tmp/dsh_home; mkdir -p $HOME`; every godot run memory-capped via `bash -c "ulimit -v 8388608; AWECRAFT_LOGIC=... timeout N /home/angrygiant/tools/godot/godot --headless --path godot"`; ONE godot at a time; never R50 or the `load` arm; no renders and never open an image; if the native lane changes, OPS.md's rebuild + re-stage is mandatory including the Windows DLL; no commits.
CONTEXT: read tasks/AC-0310/AC-0310-results.html (the tier, the thresholds, the measured stall and the owed list) and tasks/AC-0310/AC-0310-piece1-results.html (the bake, its cost and its guard).
