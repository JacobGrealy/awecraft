AC-0384 — round 4: landing on and leaving a planet should feel like No Man's Sky (honest baseline + fix the largest gap)

Goal

Capture the actual descent to, and departure from, a planet as rendered frames of the loaded, shipped Forward+ path at the user's own configuration (R24, fog full at 332 m / render edge 400 m), look at those frames (image reading is permitted this round — budget: a few decisive frames), write an honest gap list against No Man's Sky that names what already looks right as well as what does not, then fix the single largest gap if it is tractable in one turn — re-rendering to prove it — or measure it precisely enough for the next run to start from the numbers.

Method (kept from round 3): every claim about how something looks comes from a render of the loaded game read with vision — never from an arm's fields. The arms measure radii and they have been right every time; the pixel path is what has been wrong twice already.

A transition, not a frame. A single altitude proves nothing: the capture is an altitude ladder — several frames descending, and the same ladder reversed for departure (a static world: direction is a viewing order, not a game state; the ladder is captured once and read in both orders, stated explicitly in the results). AWECRAFT_CAM=planet + AWECRAFT_SAT_ALT (the round-3-proven instrument: frozen player at the camera position, HUD hidden, body visibility + uniforms ride the player's altitude).

The ladder (user config throughout)

Common envs: seed 44 (res path — no 13-min bake), AWECRAFT_SIZE=1280,720, AWECRAFT_RADIUS=24, AWECRAFT_FOG_PCT=83 (fog_far 332 / edge 400 — the user's short fog), AWECRAFT_TIME=0.5, AWECRAFT_SAT_MESH_STEP=64 (settled frame must complete on llvmpipe), AWECRAFT_SNAP_DRAIN=1200, fog ON, clouds ON, no AWECRAFT_LOGIC, no MENU_BOOT, no NO_FOG, no CLOUDS=0 (the AC-0396 recipe's NO_FOG/CLOUDS=0 existed to sidestep the fog-erase bug — fixed in r3 — so the user's fog and clouds are in every frame).

Judgement list (each item judged from the frames, NMS as the bar)

  • Atmosphere limb + fade — the NMS signature: a bright blue glowing rim around the planet disc, strongest seen from space, fading into the blue haze at lower altitude.
  • Haze transition as the surface resolves — fog wall 332 m → edge 400 m: does the terrain dissolve into the haze continuously (no hard line) as it resolves? Does the body's rim emerge exactly at the wall?
  • Terrain LOD — do the far tiers (the avg-grid bands) POP into existence or blend in at the fog wall? Any visible seam step where a tier's H differs from its neighbour's?
  • Cloud layer — position (shells at +275/+330/+400 m), coverage (day ~68 %/41 %/37 %… per shell), character from altitude; motion is a slow Y-rotation — judged from placement/coverage, and stated honestly that a still cannot show speed.
  • Sky-to-space transition — the u_space band (500→2000 m): does the sky darken smoothly, do stars fade in, does the sun's glow die with the air (AC-0386) — any banding or hard step at 500/2000?
  • Horizon curvature and scale — R=4000 m is a small planet: the horizon sits ~1 km away at 120 m altitude. Judge how it reads against NMS's near-flat horizons (the W/R grid lock is a standing design decision — a finding, not a bug).
  • Hard cuts / flicker — disc edge against space, the star shell (320-unit re-centered shell, planet-disc occlusion), the cloud shells' silhouette, the AeroWash quad, anything popping as the ladder steps.

Files to touch

  • Captures + evidence: tasks/AC-0384/rd4-*.png (5 ladder frames + post-fix pair), tasks/AC-0384/AC-0384-results.html (new §19), tasks/AC-0384/continuity.md (journal), this plan.
  • Anticipated fix (pending the pixel read — the leading candidate): the atmospheric limb glow. At altitude the sky pass (core/aero_sky_gradient.gdshader) mixes to space_color with weight S at the zenith / S² at the horizon and carries no limb term, so the disc's edge reads as a hard line against space — while NMS's identity is the glowing rim. Fix = a limb-glow term in the sky shader: a grazing-angle function of the view ray's impact parameter about the planet centre (the same physics the body's haz_a uses), weighted by the flight-band S so the surface view (S=0) stays bit-identical, driven by new u_planet_center/u_planet_radius/u_space uniforms pushed from main.gd::_update_sky (one home, the existing AC-0386 uniform). If the frames show the largest gap elsewhere (e.g. a hard LOD pop, or the R-scale reading wrong), the fix target changes and the results page records the deviation — including the precise numbers for it if it is not tractable this turn.
