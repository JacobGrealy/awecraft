Conclusion: the defect was LIVE on the current tree (re-measured first, per the ticket's top instruction), is now FIXED by baking the column-facet transform into the ring merge, and is proven by a direct measurement of the on-screen ring mesh (every vertex moved from the flat offset onto the facet) plus a deterministic before/after render at the tier boundary. 2. Mechanism — and how it differs from the already-fixed AC-0397

The far tier (coarse LOD beyond the near field, ring-batched band B/C) is draw-batched (AC-0338): every visible slab of a (tier, 1/32-angle sector) is merged into one ArrayMesh in world coordinates on a ring MeshInstance3D that sits at the identity. The merge (World._ring_rebuild_sector) used to place each slab's arrays with a flat offset Vector3(cx*16, si*16, cz*16) and unrotated normals. But the near tiers are children of the chunk node, which carries transform = SphereMath.column_transform(cx, cz, R) — a facet placement on the R = 4000 sphere. A flat plane through the column stands above the true (curved) surface by the sagitta d²/2R, so the far tier read as raised plateaus — and it could not dip into valleys, the other half of the defect.

AC-0397 was a different surface at a different place. AC-0397 fixed the band-A materialization fill (generate_resl skip=1): the near, per-chunk, materialized surface that stood on the un-carved heightmap (up to 91 blocks proud). It drove that fill from the column's far payload (the carved top) so the near surface matched the carved ground. AC-0416 is the far, ring-batched draw placement: the geometry was already the correct carved emit (farab 1080/1080, h_mismatch 0 — the emit was never wrong); it was simply placed flat in the merge. So AC-0397 changed what height the surface stands on (data/fill), AC-0416 changes where in world space the correct surface is placed (geometry transform). They touch different code (generate_resl fill vs _ring_rebuild_sector) and neither regresses the other: the farab + genhash gates are byte-green after AC-0416.

3. What changed

  • godot/world/world.gd — _ring_rebuild_sector: per column T = SphereMath.column_transform(int(c.cx), int(c.cz), Game.planet_R), Mn = T.basis.inverse().transposed() (computed once per column). Vertices bv.append(T * Vector3(v.x, float(si*16)+v.y, v.z)); normals bn.append((Mn * n).normalized()); the water-exception surfaces (1/2) get the same T and Mn. The flat off is gone. No C++ change, no .so rebuild — GDScript-only.
  • godot/scenes/harness.gd — new permanent standing arm farhigh (the ticket's owed instrument: same-column far-vs-high, 3 legs). Calibrated so the emit-envelope gate (leg 1 d) is exact (0 tolerance, 960 cells) against the C++ h_avg_emit contract.
  • tasks/harness_data.yaml — the farhigh row (HARNESS.md §1); harness_doc.py --render/--check in sync.
  • godot/ARCHITECTURE.md — the AC-0338 ring bullet: placement convention flat → column-facet (invariant 2).

Deviation from the ticket's suggested form (stated): the ticket suggested flat_to_world(x,y,z,R). That helper picks floor(x/16) as the facet column, so a slab's v.x == 16 boundary vertex would land on the neighbour column's facet — while the near tier draws that same edge on its own column's facet (local x in [0,16]). The fix uses the slab's own column's transform (the high-tier convention), which is what keeps the far/high edges coincident at the handoff.
