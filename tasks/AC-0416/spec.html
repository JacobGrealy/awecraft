Registry notes (verbatim, quoted):

1) User Story: As a player, the middle-distance terrain should be the same ground I am standing on, just coarser. Today the low/medium tiles stand visibly ABOVE the real surface - they read as raised plateaus/mesas, "way too high" (user report 2026-10-09, live build 222b1d6, the same frame that shows the far tiers painted pale blue). The carved valleys the near tiers show are also missing at that distance. Whatever the cause, a far tier that stands proud of the ground it represents is wrong, and it must not be possible for the two representations to disagree without a gate going red.

2) Technical Details:

WHAT IS OBSERVED: in the same frame, the near (high) tiles show correctly coloured, correctly shaped terrain with carved valleys; the med/low tiles are (a) pale blue (that is AC-0415 - the manual fog on core/lod_avg.gdshader - SEPARATE, already filed) and (b) standing well ABOVE the near terrain's surface, as stepped plateaus. Two defects in one frame; this ticket owns (b) and the missing valleys.

THE REPRESENTATION, and the mechanisms that can produce a too-high surface (HYPOTHESES, to be separated by measurement, not assumed):
- H1 - THE CELL ENVELOPE. gdext/src/mesh.cpp h_avg_emit_impl builds the far grid from the payload's per-column H (mesh.cpp:3015-3024: St = max(H, sea)) and marks a cell solid unless MORE THAN HALF its sub-blocks are air (`if (pc == 0 || (TOT - pc) * 2 > TOT)`, mesh.cpp:3087). Cells are 2 blocks at the med tier (G=8) and 4 blocks at the low tier (G=4) - CELLB = 16/G, mesh.cpp:3007-3010 (the "8x8x8"/"4x4x4" names count cells per chunk, NOT metres). A cell that survives is drawn as a full box up to its top, so the emitted surface is the UPPER ENVELOPE of the cell - up to one cell proud - and any feature occupying less than half of a cell is bridged over. This predicts bounded error (<=4 blocks at low) and valley LOSS, but not "way too high".
- H2 - INDEX / MIRROR DISAGREEMENT between the payload and the consumer. The payload's layout is (y<<8)|(z<<4)|x, i.e. i = z*16 + x (gen.cpp gen_flat/gen_far, and h_avg_emit_impl's own `lz * 16 + (cx*CELLB+px)`), and faces 2-11 carry a chart mirror (sphere_math face_mirror_x / face_local_x). If any consumer - the far emit, the band-A materialization, or a face-chart path - reads the column map with x and z swapped or the mirror applied on one side only, the far tier draws a NEIGHBOURING (mirrored) column's height: arbitrary disagreement in BOTH directions, valleys appearing at the wrong place, and large errors on slopes. This fits "way too high" and the missing valleys better than H1.
- H3 - THE H CONTRACT ITSELF (AC-0387 / AC-0397). The far payload's H is supposed to be the full path's CARVED top; AC-0397 fixed band A's fill to stand on the payload H rather than the un-carved heightmap (the 91-block plateau class), and AC-0387 made gen_far run the carved_top_pass (gen.cpp:2811+ - the 8 cave-lattice fields, the aquifer table, build_carver_plan, the dens_at scan and the carver, op-for-op with gen_flat per the comments at gen.cpp:2884). If any one of those steps drifted, this class returns. Note the farab gate DOES compare the far H against the full path's own heff on a 45-column battery (h_mismatch 0 standing), so a whole-lane drift should be caught - which is evidence AGAINST H3 and FOR H2.

WHY EVERY EXISTING GATE CAN BE GREEN THROUGH THIS (the important structural point, stated in HARNESS.md's own seamcensus row): farab compares the far EMIT to a reference built the same way, meshprobe/ladder/lightstate compare emitted geometry to a same-way reference, and the h_avg_emit vs low_emit_avg identity is the same-way byte check. Nothing in the standing set compares THE FAR TIER'S OUTPUT AGAINST THE LIVE HIGH MESH FOR THE SAME COLUMN. A mis-indexed or wrongly-mirrored far grid is bit-identical to its own re-emit, so it passes everything, while being wrong in the frame. That missing comparison is the acceptance instrument for this ticket.

WHAT TO DO (in order):
1. MEASURE FIRST, at a column the user can point at: take the live HIGH mesh for a column (the near band) and the far emit for the SAME column and compare their surfaces per (x,z) - the ground truth is the high mesh, not another far reference. Do it on the home face AND on a face >= 2 (the mirror path), and on a slope (where H1 and H2 separate cleanly: H1 gives a bounded upper envelope, H2 gives an arbitrary, often large, error that changes sign between neighbours).
2. Then fix what the measurement names: H1 wants a representation decision (finer low cells, or emit the far surface from the payload's own 1 m H field instead of majority boxes - the data is finer than the mesh; the cost is vertices, ~17k far slabs at R50), H2 wants an index/mirror fix plus a same-column parity check in the arm, H3 wants the farab battery extended with the offending column.
3. Whatever the cause, add the standing instrument: a same-column far-vs-high comparison (a new arm, or a widening of farab) so this class cannot be green again.

EVIDENCE OWED: the before/after same-column numbers on the named fixture (per (x,z): high surface vs far surface, max and mean error, on the home face and a <=2 face), the fixture's frame before/after, and the standing gates unchanged (genhash 25/25 if no generation change; if the fix touches generation, the new hashes are the intentional change and must be stated as such).
