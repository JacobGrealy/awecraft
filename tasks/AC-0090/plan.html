AC-0090 — plan

delivered Three.js web prototype, built and measured · results: AC-0090-results.html · design essay: ../../docs/ragdoll-character-style.html

Goal

Determine whether a character assembled from obvious primitive shapes can be made to read as one seamless body by a custom, mobile-performant technique — toon shaded, and driven entirely by a procedural locomotion rig that covers 2-legged, any-legged, no-legged (hopping) and flying body plans.

Approach

  1. Frame the problem before building. Name the three failure modes a primitive body has — intersection crease, butt joint, and joint tear — and the four candidate fixes (implicit remesh, screen-space blur, vertex-shader SDF, accept the seams). Decide which one to build and why. Result: the tube envelope, plus the implicit remesh kept as a second tier so the comparison is a button rather than a claim.
  2. Build the body generator first, not the renderer. A plan is joints plus shapes plus locomotion wiring; everything downstream reads that structure. Five presets exercise the body-plan space.
  3. Bake the seam into the geometry and the weights, not the shader. A limb is one tapered tube spanning every joint of its chain, so a joint is one surface; the joint blend is baked into skin weights, so it costs nothing at runtime.
  4. Stand up numeric gates before trusting anything visual. Watertightness, a reference-free orientation sum, rest-pose identity, edge stretch under animation, "does every bone skin geometry", and determinism. This turned out to be the whole game — see below.
  5. Then the presentation and animation layers: toon bands, rim, inverted-hull outline, and the procedural gaits.
  6. Verify in the browser through the same API a person uses. No test-only code path; the harness drives window.__AC0090.

What actually happened

The plan's step 4 is the reason this ticket has a result at all. The prototype rendered plausible creatures through a dozen serious defects — an inverted winding fix, an identity bind pose, a double-counted FK offset, three separate weight-indexing bugs, a scratch buffer that aliased its own input, an inverted superellipsoid bound, and finally a body mesh that was not being drawn at all because its model matrix was never assigned. Every one of them survived a screenshot. The gates found most of them; the last one was found only by bypassing the screenshot and reading the shader's uniforms. The writeup records all twelve in full.

Explicitly out of scope

  • Any Godot code. This is a web prototype; godot/ was not touched.
  • Ragdoll physics. "Ragdoll-like" here describes the look — loose, primitive-composed joints.
  • Device performance measurement. The frame times in the results are from a software rasteriser and are labelled as such.
  • A shipping-quality rig: there is no general IK solver, no LOD system and no automatic LOD.
