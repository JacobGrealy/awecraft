AC-0443 — attribute the unexplained dark patch at the horizon

Goal

Attribute the flat warm-dark region (≈(30,27,23), ≈100×30 px) at the right horizon of tasks/AC-0384/ac0384-r24-ground-west-day.png — behind a sheep — by geometric correspondence (locate the thing in the live scene, project it with the camera that took the frame, match the observed region to within 2 px on all four edges; the AC-0440 standard), not by resemblance. Then: if it is a defect, fix it and re-take the frame; if it is a legitimate object, judge whether its darkness is itself correct (a midday object at 30/255 would be the AC-0432 lighting-collapse class — a defect even though the object is real).

Attribution attempt 1 (static, no engine) — what it found

  • Patch measured on the r24 frame: flat, uniform, warm-dark (25–30 across channels), left edge crisp/vertical, top edge soft (a blurred ~1-cell bright band), extends to the right frame edge (x≈1150–1279, y≈363–400; clipped, so ≥90×37 px ≈ ≥37 m × ≥14 m at the 332 m fog wall). A white sheep stands in front at ≈(1218,402).
  • The camera (AIM recipe, yaw 1.57 = west, right = north) puts the patch on the north-west horizon. At the fog wall (332 m) the near tiers discard their fragments (AC-0401 partition at u_fog_far) and the near terrain is 100% fog colour — bright. A flat dark region there therefore cannot be near-tier terrain. Candidates narrow to: (a) the satellite body (far tier) drawing dark pixels at that direction, (b) a hole in the near tier that shows the body/sky behind, (c) a legitimate dark object (tree/animal) — unlikely: the shape is geometric, flat, clipped, and the ambient lift (AC-0432) keeps floor faces at L≈0.61 at noon.
  • Body-shader reading (noon, T=0.5): the horizon-facing normal gets sun_l≈1 → light factor 1.0, so a body patch at (30,27,23) implies baked texels that are near-black at that (u,v) — a bake/data question, or the haze blend (haz_d toward u_air) is not the cause (that is bright). The static pass cannot decide which face/cell the patch ray hits — the live probe is owed.
  • Recent-work check (ticket item): since the r24 tree (56c9095) the queue ran AC-0441 (cloud jitter — clouds only, the patch is not a cloud), AC-0442 (low-handoff guard — a skipped emit leaves a slab PENDING, whose hole would look exactly like this; the fix changed bookkeeping only, and holes at the wall render as bright fog/body by the no-pop design — so a pending slab is a candidate cause of a hole, not of a dark body pixel). The uncommitted fence files (drop/held-item + cloud-look work) are drop.gd/main.gd/game.gd/debug.gd — none of them own the body or the near-tier mesh path. Re-take on the current tree is still owed (a hole from a pending slab is transient; a dark bake is standing).

Attribution attempt 2 (the probe) — design

Temporary arm ac0443probe in godot/scenes/harness.gd (in and out in this task; pristine rule). Headless run, exact r24 state: seed 44, AWECRAFT_RADIUS=24, AWECRAFT_FOG_PCT=83 (fog_far=332/render_edge=400), AWECRAFT_TIME=0.5, AWECRAFT_TIME_FREEZE=1, AWECRAFT_CLOUD_T0=411200, then the r24 ground-west AIM state verbatim (Debug.fly(true), Debug.teleport(8.5, 143.62−EYE, 8.5), player.look(1.57, 0.08), _await_world_build 600 frames + satellite-LOADED wait + pose re-pin + 8 frames + time re-pin — the _snapshot_finish sequence). Then, for a pixel grid spanning the patch bbox and control points:

  1. Live camera: cam.global_position (world + flat), basis, fov (fly-boosted ≈99°), aspect 1280/720 — the AC-0440 “live transform”.
  2. Near tier: DDA in the flat frame (world.get_block, the _rayline pattern) — first solid cells + distance per ray; for every chunk the ray crosses to ≈450 m: data present? mesh_built? — the pending-slab/hole census.
  3. Satellite body: analytic exit of the ray from the displaced surface (rad(s) = |P+s·d − C| vs R + H(u,v), H from satellite._hgrid_load via SatelliteBakeWorker.hgrid_sample + SphereMath.world_to_face; 2 m scan + bisection). At each inside→outside crossing: face, (u,v), H, the baked texel RGB+alpha from satellite.textures[face] (±8 px neighbourhood census), the shader lighting prediction at that normal (sun_l, light factor, haz, predicted albedo), and the 2 m inset note.
  4. Mobs: every entity’s center() + key, world/flat pos, distance, projected pixel — locates the sheep and checks occlusion order.

Python (offline, PIL) then projects the identified geometry (exit point, the body cell’s corner vertices, the suspect chunk’s footprint, the sheep) with the live camera and matches the observed patch edges (±2 px on all four sides, the AC-0440 standard).
