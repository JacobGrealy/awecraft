## Coordinator triage — stage L1: the face lane carries a payload it never uses (round 57)
- THE HEADLINE FINDING IS NOT ABOUT OWNERSHIP AT ALL: the face path's "re-bake on the payload" DISCARDS the payload. `build_mesh_face` requires an `arr` key (chunk.gd:1995) while `slab_light_payload` produces `eff` (starlight.cpp:822-884) and never sets `pl["star"]=true`, so face mesh light is ALWAYS an own-column pull and the engine is only a change-detector plus a `last_eff` bookkeeper. The home path by contrast treats the payload as authoritative (AC-0358: applied bake == settled payload == fresh same-input reference). So the two lanes do not merely have two engines - they have two different LIGHTING RULES, and the face one is the weaker: the builder notes the own-column pull is neighbour-blind, a visible divergence.
- THAT CHANGES THE STAKES OF THIS TICKET: it is not tidiness. Unifying the feed would give every face the payload-backed, AC-0358-corrected light that only the home pair gets today. The cost-of-not-doing-it answer is therefore "cheap in CPU, but the face world lights differently from the home world" - which is precisely the kind of two-experiences-one-planet outcome this migration exists to remove.
- THE CONSTRAINT THAT WILL BITE, named concretely: the payload's w_lo/w_hi strip window (2-row overhang, starlight.cpp:797-803) plus zero-filled/unseeded-zero strips must be EXACTLY what the bake consumes - the window, not the light, drives the applied mesh (AC-0358 C4). A unified feed that reshapes the window silently changes mesh light.
