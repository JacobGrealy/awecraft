AC-0463 stage L7 — what do the per-face engines still provide after L3 and L6?

Coordinator contract. Medium builder applies it with tasks/templates/implement.md. Diagnosis only — no code change. Step 5 of L1's order is "delete _star_faces". Before deleting, establish precisely what it still does, because L1's list of its defects was written before L3 removed the O(faces×owed) re-scan and L6 made the build consume the payload.

1. Why this stage exists

My last two predictions about this subsystem were both wrong: L1 said the face light was visibly divergent (measured zero, and L6 confirmed steady-state light is unchanged), and L5 said the payload could not be obtained for faces (it could; it simply was not consumed). A deletion based on a third guess is not worth the risk, especially since the engines are the thing that knows when light has settled, and settling drives remeshing.

2. What to establish (each with file:line evidence)

  • 2.1 The remaining function. After L3 (one drain entry, one dispatch), what does a per-face AweStarlight instance still do that nothing else does? Walk its callers: the step, the drain, last_eff publishing, the owed/evict bookkeeping, the settled signal that triggers a remesh.
  • 2.2 What consumes the settled signal on faces. Which decision would be made wrongly if the per-face engines vanished — the remesh trigger, the E2 re-arm, the compare-and-erase? Name the site and say what it would do instead if there were no per-face engine.
  • 2.3 Per-face state that cannot be world-wide. L1 classified some things as genuinely per-face (the key space, mirror-class orientation, the AC-0297 pull-landing rule). Is any of that engine state, or is it all chunk/mesh state that survives without a per-face engine?
  • 2.4 The three candidate end states, costed. (a) DELETE the per-face engines and let the home engine own all twelve faces — say what breaks and what the migration would need to gain first. (b) KEEP them as settle-trackers, deleting only the residue L1 listed (the redundant re-scan is already gone; is anything else left?). (c) A middle option if one exists. Cost each in code touched and in risk, and recommend one with reasons.
  • 2.5 If (b) wins, is there anything left to delete at all? If the answer is "nothing", say so — an empty step 5 closes this ticket on a different footing than a deletion would, and that is worth knowing before a builder is turned loose.

6. Risks

The honest answer may be "keep them" or "there is nothing left to delete". Both are acceptable and better than a deletion justified by a guess. A note recommending deletion without naming what consumes the settled signal would be worse than no note.
