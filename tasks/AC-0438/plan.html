3. The two candidate fixes (choose with evidence)

  1. (A) SHADER-ONLY per-face radial offset. Cheap, no asset change, no genhash impact. Correct if the seam is a geometric discontinuity at the chart boundary — two adjacent chart faces meeting with a small radial/height step that a per-face offset can cancel.
  2. (B) BAKE-WORKER seam-UV nudge. Correct if the seam is in the baked texture — adjacent charts sampling texels that disagree at the boundary. THIS OWES A DELIBERATE GENHASH REBASE, so it is the more expensive option and must be justified, not defaulted to.

The test that decides: the seam's character distinguishes them. A step that persists when the texture is bypassed (or that moves only with geometry) points at (A); a step that changes with the bake or vanishes on a flat-coloured body points at (B). Measure, then choose — do not implement both.

3a. ⚠ REPAIR (round 113) — BOTH (A) AND (B) ARE REFUTED. THE CAUSE IS (C), THE SAMPLING PATH.

A medium run measured all of this and correctly returned PLAN-INSUFFICIENT instead of forcing a fix. Its evidence, which is the starting point of this repair:

  • THE SEAM IS STILL PRESENT on the current tree, same character as filed: rows 357↔358, n=415, mean_abs 45.0, max 212; the line pixel reads (179,210,95) at x=640 against neighbours (41,157,0). So AC-0469 D and AC-0478 did not remove it, and the re-measure requirement is satisfied.
  • (A) GEOMETRY IS REFUTED: a flat-body probe makes the line VANISH (n=372 → 0 of 681, max 0). The artifact therefore depends on texture content, not on geometry.
  • MINIFICATION IS REFUTED: with the mip chain OFF the line is UNCHANGED (n=356, max 119), so it is not a mip level.
  • (B) BAKE CONTENT IS REFUTED BY A STATIC FACT: the boundary columns f00[i=0] ≡ f01[i=1023] are 973 of 1024 texels BYTE-IDENTICAL, max channel delta 31 (quantization wobble only, no bright/dark pair), in both bakes. There is no content mismatch for a bake nudge to fix.
  • AND THE DECISIVE CLUE: the line's colour (179,210,95) exists in NO TEXEL (max R in the atlas is 250). A colour that exists nowhere in the source but is drawn on screen is the signature of a FILTERED BLEND ACROSS A BOUNDARY — the sampler is averaging texels that are not true neighbours.

THEREFORE THE CAUSE IS (C): THE SAMPLING PATH AT THE CHART BOUNDARY — the UV derivation or the address mode at the seam, with linear/anisotropic filtering blending across it. The bake is consistent and the geometry is not involved; what is wrong is which texels the boundary pixel samples.

THINGS TO CHECK FOR (C), in this order:

  1. The address mode / wrap on the satellite textures. A wrap that is not clamp — or a clamp that is correct on one axis and not the other — makes a boundary pixel sample the opposite edge of the atlas, which at a 1024-texel face is a very different colour and blends to something no texel contains.
  2. The UV derivation at the face boundary: does the UV reach exactly 1.0 at the last column, or overshoot past it? An overshoot of half a texel samples the next chart (or the opposite edge).
  3. The filter setting, now that AC-0412 re-enabled filter_linear_mipmap_anisotropic + generate_mipmaps(): anisotropy samples along the view-major axis and can reach further across a boundary than plain linear. Compare with anisotropy forced off — but note mips-off did NOT change it, so do not assume the filter alone is the cause; test anisotropy separately from the mip chain.
  4. The atlas layout: are the twelve faces in ONE atlas texture (in which case a linear sample at a face edge legitimately touches the neighbouring face and must be clamped per-face), or twelve separate textures (in which case wrap/clamp per texture is the knob)? Establish which, because the fix differs.

ACCORDINGLY: implementing (A) or (B) is now forbidden by evidence. Implement (C). If (C)'s four checks all come back clean and the line survives, then return PLAN-INSUFFICIENT with what you ruled out — a fourth option will need new evidence to name.
