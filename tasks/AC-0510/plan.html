AC-0510 — remove the fog and the mist; chunks emerge from the satellite LOD

Coordinator contract. Medium builder applies it with tasks/templates/implement.md. A removal ticket with a proof obligation: if something was being hidden, report the thing rather than restoring the fog.

1. The ruling

User, with screenshots: "I don't think the fog or mist make sense any more, remove both of them. the chunks should appear out of the satellite lod not blue mist."

The observation that makes this more than a settings flip: with fog ON, the LODs that load in are blue while flying, but the planet satellite is not — the two tiers are being atmospherically treated differently. And with fog OFF the chunks still appear out of a blue mist, which means a second atmospheric term exists independently of the user-facing fog setting. Both must go.

2. The two effects, already located

  • THE FOG — settings.gd:401 "fog_enabled": true (user-facing, already toggleable), plus "fog_start_pct": 87 at :400, the distance model DayNight.fog_far(render_radius, fog_start_pct), and the push site around world.gd's fog uniform set.
  • THE MIST / WASH — a separate effect: main.gd:1187 creates an AeroWash quad with core/aero_wash.gdshader only if AeroLib.wash_on(), and main.gd:1277 sets aero_wash.visible = true. Its uniforms are WASH_COLOR, WASH_AMOUNT, WASH_TOP_GLOW. This is why turning fog off leaves the mist — it is a full-screen haze quad gated by a different switch.
  • A POSSIBLE THIRD: core/lod_avg.gdshader carries the far tier's own haze terms (haz_b, which AC-0399 repaired after finding the mix reversed). If the far tier keeps haze while the near tier loses it, the tiers still will not match — check it in §3c.

3. What to do

  1. 3a REMOVE THE FOG. Default fog_enabled to false and make the push path not apply fog when it is off. Decide and state whether the setting stays as an opt-in or is removed outright — the user said "remove", so if you keep it as a toggle say why, and if you remove it say what happens to a saved cfg that sets it (a saved user://awecraft.cfg can still request it; handle that explicitly rather than leaving it undefined).
  2. 3b REMOVE THE MIST. Make AeroLib.wash_on() false / drop the wash quad and its shader use. Same cfg question: state how a saved override is handled.
  3. 3c CHECK THE FAR TIER'S HAZE (lod_avg.gdshader's haz_b and its mix). If it survives, the far tier still has atmosphere the near tier does not, which is the asymmetry the user is describing. Report whether it stays and why — do not silently remove a term AC-0399 measured and repaired without saying so.
  4. 3d PROVE THE REPLACEMENT BEHAVIOUR. The user wants chunks to appear out of the satellite LOD. With both terms gone, the far tier already draws those pixels, so the transition should be the far tier's appearance resolving into geometry with no atmospheric overlay between them. Verify that at the streaming frontier and report what you see with numbers (pixel values at the frontier, before and after).

4. THE PROOF OBLIGATION — the part that matters most

Fog and wash may be load-bearing. They commonly hide: (a) unbuilt-chunk edges at the streaming frontier, (b) the aerial perspective that makes altitude readable, (c) the far tier's haze, and (d) the handover between tiers (AC-0451). AC-0451's own measurement found the aerial see-through was mostly UNBUILT CHUNKS rather than the handover — which is exactly the kind of defect fog was covering.

So: remove them, then LOOK for what they were hiding. If removal exposes a pop, a hard unbuilt edge, or a visible gap at the frontier, THAT DEFECT IS THE REAL ONE — report it as a finding. DO NOT reinstate fog or wash to hide it, and do not reduce the removal to a partial one to avoid the question.

8. Risks

(1) Reinstating fog to hide a pop — the ticket forbids it; report the pop instead. (2) Removing only the user-facing fog and leaving the wash, which is the exact bug the user already observed. (3) Leaving the far tier's haz_b in place so the tiers still differ. (4) Forgetting a saved cfg can re-request either effect. (5) Silently changing the lightstate arm's standing value without re-baselining it.
