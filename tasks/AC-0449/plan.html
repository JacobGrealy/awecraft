Elimination verdict (PROVEN by the v1/v2 A/B): #1, the engine DOF. In the user's 941 m nadir view the whole visible disc (943 m at the frame centre → 2545 m at the limb arcs) sits past the 87.62 m wall, so every visible fragment renders at the maximum blur: "completely blurred out even things right in front of you". Instrument note (why the planet preset could not see it)

The planet preset's shot camera is a separate Camera3D (main._make_camera(), fov 75, no attributes — harness.gd:1349-1387; the AC-0421 comment names it). The preset spawns a frozen player only so the satellite body's altitude uniforms ride on it — the snapshot never goes through the player camera, so DoF is never applied in planet-preset frames. A pristine-tree DoF-on vs DoF-off planet shot came back byte-identical, proving it. All planet-preset captures in the tree (a0/a0f/a1/a2/n0/…) are DoF-free. The elimination therefore runs on the default cam path with AWECRAFT_AIM=8.5,941,8.5,0,±pitch — the player teleported to the 941 m radial, snapshot through the player camera (the user's actual camera, DoF live).

Why the near field is affected (the mechanism, per the correction's list)

Not an inverted depth term and not a full-screen kernel — it is a wrong focus/far distance: the blur zone begins at 82.62 m, a value calibrated for a grounded player. At 941 m altitude the "near field" (the terrain in front, 943 m at the nadir) is farther than the far wall, so a correct distance-based blur blurs it at full strength. The effect is correct by construction; its distance is wrong for a world whose user flies.
