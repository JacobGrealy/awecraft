AC-0479 — the aim box is axis-aligned in a warped world, and the player slides on the tilt

Coordinator contract. Medium builder applies it with tasks/templates/implement.md. Two symptoms, one suspected root cause (the warp) — but the plan requires the magnitude test before any fix.

1. The report

User, with a screenshot: "the block selector does not match 1 to 1 with the blocks, and the player slides in the direction that the selection block is angled." In the capture the black wireframe box is clearly skewed relative to the terrain and offset from the block it should outline.

2. What the code already tells us — the mechanism, located

  • The box is placed by position only. player.gd:3177-3190 (_update_interaction) does hl.global_position = Game.world.world_pos_of_flat(cell.x+0.5, cell.y+0.5, cell.z+0.5) and nothing else: no basis, no rotation. Note its own comment — "the aimed cell is FLAT; the highlight node lives in the placed global frame" — which is true of the position and says nothing about the orientation.
  • The box's own geometry is axis-aligned. _aim_wire_t(sx,sy,sz,ox,oy,oz) (player.gd:1164) builds each of the 12 edge strips from a scale and an offset only — a perfectly axis-aligned frame at the 1.02 extent.
  • But the terrain it must match is NOT axis-aligned any more. The warp (AC-0458-0462) displaces vertices, so a block's faces are tilted and slightly curved in world space, and collision consumes those warped vertices (AC-0461). So within the warp's non-identity region an axis-aligned box cannot coincide with a tilted block — that is not a rounding error, it is a shape mismatch, which is exactly what "does not match 1 to 1" describes.
  • The slide follows the same tilt. If the ground under the player is tilted by the warp while the walk code assumes axis-aligned floors (the pre-warp assumption: gravity down, ground normal up), a player standing still drifts along the tilt's downhill direction — and the drift would run in the direction the box appears angled, because both are the same warp gradient.

So the working hypothesis is ONE cause with two symptoms: the warp applied to geometry and collision but not to the aim indicator's orientation or the ground-walk assumption. It must be tested, not assumed — §4.1.

3. What to establish FIRST (in this order)

  1. 3.1 THE MAGNITUDE TEST — it decides everything. Record the box's misalignment at two places with known-different warp magnitude (near the warp's identity region and far from it). If the error grows with warp magnitude, the diagnosis in §2 holds. If the error is constant (e.g. a consistent half-cell or whole-cell offset), it is a plain coordinate/rounding bug and §2 is wrong — say so and stop, do not fix a warp problem that is not there.
  2. 3.2 Characterise the misalignment shape: is the box offset by half a cell, a whole cell, or rotated/skewed? The capture suggests skewed, which supports §2, but state what you measure.
  3. 3.3 Measure the slide: stand still on flat-looking ground at two warp magnitudes and record how far and in which direction the player drifts per second. If the drift is zero near the identity region and non-zero elsewhere, that is the same confirmation from a second, independent instrument — two symptoms agreeing is much stronger than either alone.

4. Then fix, minimally

  1. 4.1 The box's orientation. The box must match the block's actual warped orientation. Two acceptable approaches — choose and justify: derive the local basis from the warp's Jacobian at the cell (sample world_pos_of_flat at the cell centre and at ±ε along each axis, build the basis from the differences), or, if that is numerically unstable, the honest fallback is to state that an axis-aligned indicator cannot match a warped block and propose the alternative. Do not proceed by scaling or offsetting the box to "fit better" — a fitted constant is the mask this session has repeatedly refused.
  2. 4.2 The slide. Whatever makes the walk code handle a tilted ground normal instead of assuming flat. The minimal honest fix is whichever the measurement in 3.3 names — do not retune friction to hide a drift, because a friction change would slow the player everywhere to compensate for a defect somewhere.

7. Acceptance

The magnitude test reported with numbers and the diagnosis confirmed or refuted; if confirmed, the box oriented to the warped block and the slide removed, each measured before/after; if refuted, the real cause named from the constant-error evidence instead.

8. Risks

(1) Assuming the warp without the magnitude test — the plan's whole guard. (2) A fitted constant offset that makes the screenshot look better in one spot. (3) Masking the slide with friction or speed, which would degrade movement everywhere for a local defect. (4) Fixing the box and the slide as two unrelated bugs when they may be one — the two-instrument confirmation in 3.1/3.3 exists to settle that.
