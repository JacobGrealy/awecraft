Root cause (the coordinator's hypothesis, confirmed in the frame): a sky term evaluated against a fixed state rather than the player's local one. The AC-0384-r8 contract made the fog seam exact by construction at ground level: the world's near tiers fade to the single-source air colour u_air (env fog fog_light_color = the body's u_air = the sky's horizon value — "one colour at the seam"). AC-0417/AC-0419 then re-keyed the sky to the local zenith with the A window w = Aero.sky_space(alt_rad), and space-thinned the below-horizon branch with the same w — so at any altitude where w > 0, the sky's horizon value and its below-horizon fill drift away from the fog colour the world is still dissolving into. The seam, exact at w = 0, becomes a hard edge at altitude. The step(0.0, -hl) switch is the same boundary drawn twice: above the line the sky is space-thinned, below it the flat space-thinned fill, and the two differ by up to 64/255 in one pixel row. It is not a world-aligned plane (the sky is correctly keyed to the local radial — upc = -u_limb_center/r); it is a drawn boundary between two regions that no longer share a colour at the player's altitude — the fourth instance of the flat-world class, in its fog/haze form.

Why it survived the previous sky fixes: AC-0419's acceptance views were the ground dome, the zenith (looking up) and the limb (looking at the tangent) — all radial or tangential views. The user's frame is the one view none of them covers: looking at the local horizon from altitude, where both the horizon step and the fog-wall/body-rim seam sit in the frame.

2. The fix (one switch, default ON, shader default OFF = the old path bit-for-bit)

All in sky_on(), behind the new u_horizon (Settings "sky_horizon", default ON — the user asked for the edge removed, twice; the pre-fix look is the shader default, so a material that never sees the push keeps the old look; A/B via AWECRAFT_SKYHORIZON=0|1, the AWECRAFT_RAMPS pattern).
