Registry notes (verbatim, quoted):

1) User Story: From high in the atmosphere or in space the player should see the planet - its curve, its air, its weather - and not patches of the ground they just left. At the moment the finest detail disappears with altitude but the middle and coarser ground detail does not, so looking down from height still shows terrain that should have given way to the planet view.

2) Technical Details:
REQUESTED BY THE USER 2026-10-07, in their words: "the medium and low lods should also disappear at height along with the high". This extends AC-0401's fourth item, which hid only the HIGH detail level above a threshold and was verified only for that tier - its acceptance measured high detail present at 120 and 300 m and absent at 700, 1600 and 2600 m, and said nothing about the middle and coarse tiers because nobody had asked yet.
WHAT TO BUILD: every near tier - high, medium and low - should give way to the satellite body as altitude rises, on one consistent rule rather than three thresholds that can drift apart. AC-0401's mechanism is the pattern to extend: it used the body's own fog wall as the threshold, per fragment, so the body and the near detail could never both vanish or both show. Extend that same single-source rule across all three tiers so there is exactly one altitude at which the world hands over to the planet view, and so the tiers disappear in a sensible order rather than the coarse one outliving the fine one. If the honest answer is that a tier is better hidden by distance than by altitude, say so and explain why rather than forcing the metaphor.
WATCH FOR THE INTERACTION WITH WHAT JUST SHIPPED: AC-0402 made the sky respond to altitude, gated behind a switch, with the limb glow, stars and sun glow kept on the physics term. The handover from ground to space is now a sequence - near detail, then cloud deck, then atmosphere, then space - and this ticket changes one step of it. Check the whole sequence on the ladder afterwards, not just the tier you changed.
ACCEPTANCE IS THE LADDER, at the user's configuration (radius 24, fog fully opaque at 332 m) across 2600, 1600, 700, 300 and 120 m, for a descent and ideally a takeoff: at each altitude say which tiers and shells are drawn and which are not, measured from the frames rather than read from the code, and show clearly that above the handover the only thing visible is the planet and its atmosphere. Round 12's captures ac0384-final-descent-*.png in tasks/AC-0384/ and AC-0402's ladder captures are the before set.
CONSTRAINTS: genhash 25/25 byte-identical; G0 zero SCRIPT ERROR and zero SHADER ERROR via tasks/scripts/gate_census.py - AND note that a clean census is not sufficient, because a run can abort with zero errors in the log, so check the exit status too; the standing set green (farab h_mismatch 0, ladder 20/20, meshprobe 1.0, halo ok, lightstate 0/0, r16 all sub-flags with far_holes 0/0 both bands, boundary r4 in band, shaderforce 12/12); the cloud shell radii and centre stay 4275/4330/4400 about (0, -R, 0); AC-0401's cloud look, near-field fog-only fade and limb gate must keep working, AC-0402's altitude-aware sky must keep working, AC-0399's view-weighted fill and AC-0398's interpolated lighting must keep working, and AC-0400's seam fade must keep targeting the single-source air colour.


SECOND ITEM ADDED BY THE USER 2026-10-07, in their words: "the limb effect is still being applied to the low lods, it should not be applied to low, medium, or high lods, only the satellite lod." This is a correction to AC-0401, which gated the glow BY ALTITUDE and verified it that way - measured S = 1.000 at 2600 m falling to 0.000 at 300 and 120 m - and the coordinator reported that as satisfying the request. It did not, and the reason is worth stating precisely because it is the whole diagnosis: THE GATE CONTROLS WHEN THE GLOW IS DRAWN, NOT WHICH MATERIAL CARRIES IT. With the gate open at altitude, the glow is still applied to the near-terrain materials, so the low, medium and high tiers show a limb effect they should never have. THE FIX IS PER-MATERIAL, NOT PER-ALTITUDE: the atmospheric limb treatment belongs to the satellite body's material alone, and the near tiers must have no limb term in any state - not gated off at some altitudes, absent from the shader path entirely - so that no future change to an altitude gate can bring it back. Verify by measuring the rim on the NEAR TIERS specifically at an altitude where the gate is open, which is the case the previous acceptance never checked: its ladder measured the glow from orbit and found it correct there, and measured the near field at low altitude where the gate was already shut. The missing case is the near field seen from altitude, and that is exactly what the user is looking at.
This is the third round in a row where the user's report has been more precise than the coordinator's acceptance criteria: the gate passed its own test and failed the user's use. When the fix lands, the ladder must be checked for the near tiers at EVERY altitude including the high ones.

THIRD ITEM ADDED BY THE USER 2026-10-07, in their words: "also the clouds still look 2d not voluminous." This is a correction to AC-0401, which was asked for "less of them but the individual clouds should be bigger and they should be volumetric", delivered fewer-and-larger convincingly (the field at a third of its previous frequency, band gaps, puff-and-core shading, verified at 120 m where the old frame was a fine membrane filling the sky and the new one is a few large soft masses with sky visible between them) - and did not achieve the volume the user is asking for.
THE LIKELY REASON, to be verified rather than assumed, and it is structural rather than a matter of shading detail: ONE SPHERICAL SHELL CANNOT LOOK VOLUMETRIC. A single surface at a single radius has no parallax and no internal depth, so however good its pattern is, the eye reads a painted membrane - which is exactly the phrase the round 12 assessment used for it (D1: "a flat patterned membrane, not volumetric cloud"). THE WORLD ALREADY HAS THREE SHELLS at radii 4275, 4330 and 4400 with different heights and coverage values, and they read as one deck because they are drawn as one. USE THEM AS DEPTH LAYERS INSTEAD: give each its own pattern phase and scale so the layers differ, so that a mass seen through a gap in a nearer layer is visibly behind it, and so that a descent passes through several distinct layers rather than one. That is where parallax and therefore volume comes from, and it costs no new geometry - the shells and their verified radii must not move.
THE OTHER HALF OF VOLUME IS LIGHT. Flat clouds look flat because every part of them is the same brightness. Real cloud reads as a volume because the sun-facing side is bright, the underside and the side away from the sun are dark, and the transition between them is gradual. AC-0401 improved the shading (puff-and-core) but the round 12 assessment recorded exactly this deficit with numbers: the deck's brightness spread measured 16.9 against 32.4 for the reference look, and its colour was near-pure grey, 222,222,222, where the reference carries saturation, 181,230,194. So: widen the brightness range, let the sun direction drive it, and consider whether the deck should carry some of the air colour rather than being neutral.
ACCEPTANCE: the same 120 m frame and the same 2600 m frame the user is judging, plus a middle altitude, compared against AC-0401's captures - and the brightness spread measured against the same instrument the assessment used, so the improvement is a number and not an impression. If a vertical or multi-layer approach is taken, show a frame in which one layer is visibly behind another.
CONSTRAINTS: the cloud shell radii and centre stay exactly 4275/4330/4400 about (0, -R, 0), because they are verified correct; keep AC-0401's cloud switch working with its off state bit-identical; the near-field fog-only fade, the altitude-gated limb on the body alone, AC-0402's altitude-aware sky, AC-0399's view-weighted fill and AC-0398's interpolated lighting must all keep working.
