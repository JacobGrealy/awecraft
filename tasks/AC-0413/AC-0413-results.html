3. What the recorded reading actually was (the cause question, answered)

The ticket’s caution turned out to be the ticket. The finding frames (r19, r21) came from the inverted-winding tree; the r19 assessment had already separated the components, and this round re-measures the fixed side of that separation:

  • The flat wash over the disc was the body’s underside, not the deck. r19: the wash was byte-identical with clouds on or off (so not the deck) and gone with SAT_BODY=0 (so the body). AC-0411 fixed the winding; the disc now shows the baked terrain (the green continent, snow, water in §2’s frames).
  • The “nearly invisible tint” over the disc is now measured nil (§2): in the sustained-deck state the columns over the disc are partly cloudy — masses in the ring band, gaps over the disc showing the surface. The user story’s ask — “masses, gaps, depth” rather than a flat ring — is exactly what the current frames show: lumpy masses with lit tops and dark gaps in the ring (23 % of the 2600 m deck pixels below luminance 60 = the gaps reading as black sky), the band crossing the disc at 1600 m, and the full internal structure inside the layer (§4).
  • The step-density hypothesis is not the cause. Tested against the measurement rather than assumed: at the shipped 24 view steps/segment the ring already resolves mass structure from orbit (spread 68.0, real gaps — the 791 m deck period is ~8× oversampled even on the longest ~2400 m limb chord, and the in-scatter is dominated by the near side of the chord where the chord is short). And the region the hypothesis would have flattened — the over-disc film — is not a saturated uniform in-scatter in this state but transparent (nil ON/OFF delta): there is no flat accumulation for coarser steps to have smoothed. If a weather state existed in which the over-disc deck read as a flat film, that would be the defect to bisect; the sustained-deck state — the one every cloud round uses as the canonical dense state — does not show it.
