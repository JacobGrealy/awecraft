AC-0473 stage M1 — can the 1 m grid fill a 400 m radius at all?

Coordinator contract. Medium builder applies it with tasks/templates/implement.md. Measurement only — no code change. The ticket's first task is to establish the RATE and, crucially, whether the required chunk set is reachable at any plausible rate. That answer decides whether this is an optimisation ticket or a scale decision for the user.

1. What is already known (do not re-derive)

  • Current state (AC-0451 P2, measured on the current tree): at render radius 24 the world never settles and runs 0.5–1.3 FPS.
  • Per-chunk costs (earlier work): generation ~13.4 ms, mesh ~19.2 ms — about 33 ms of work per chunk.
  • Ordering is already fixed and is not the limit: AC-0399's view bias landed (71115ad) and its FLIP negative test proved the instrument reads order, not rate. The ground order is md5-identical. Do not re-litigate ordering.
  • The grid: 1.000 m cubic cells, chunk = 16 cells = 16 m, render radius 24 chunks = 384–400 m.

2. What to measure (numbers, in a table)

  • 2.1 The required set. How many chunks must be resident at R24 under the current policy — the stream set, both lanes, at a standing position and at a mid-flight position. Report the number, not an estimate from a formula alone.
  • 2.2 The achieved rate. Chunks built per second, measured over a real fill from cold: time to reach 25%, 50%, 90% of the required set, and whether it converges at all within the arm's run. Do this on the ground and at the altitude where the user sees the defect.
  • 2.3 Where the time goes. Split the per-chunk cost between generation, mesh build, landing and any main-thread drain, as measured (not from earlier notes — re-measure, because the grid changed since those numbers).
  • 2.4 The arithmetic verdict. Required chunks ÷ achieved rate = seconds to fill. Compare against a plausible desktop rate (state the multiplier you assume and why). Then answer plainly: is the R24 set reachable in, say, under a minute on a desktop, or is it out of reach by an order of magnitude?
  • 2.5 What the levers would buy. For each of (a) lower render radius, (b) larger far-tier handover, (c) coarser LOD beyond some distance: how much does the required set shrink, as a factor. This is a table the user can decide from, not a recommendation to implement. Block size is fixed and must not appear as a lever.

6. Risks

Do not optimise anything in this stage, and do not recommend a lever as if the decision were yours: the lever choice is the user's, which is why §2.5 is a table of what each would buy rather than a recommendation. Report the gap honestly if an existing arm cannot give a number you need.
