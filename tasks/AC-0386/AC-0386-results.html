2. What changed (and which curve, and why)

2.1 The transition curve — the flight band's own smoothstep, verbatim

Decision: the sky is driven by the SAME smoothstep(500, 2000, alt_rad) the flight model uses — not a different curve. Reasons: (a) the ticket's core ask is that the visual handover and the control handover agree instead of being two independent thresholds — a second curve would re-introduce exactly the split being fixed; (b) the flight curve is already C¹ smooth (smoothstep) — it cannot pop, so no deliberate alternative is needed; (c) the constants stay a single fact with one home (player.gd): main.gd reads player.BAND_WALK_MAX / player.BAND_FLY_MIN off the live player instance — no duplicated numbers that could drift apart. The sky completes going dark at exactly the altitude (2000 m) where the player enters the 6-DOF band — the moment of leaving lands with the controls, not before or after.

2.2 Sky pass — core/aero_sky_gradient.gdshader + main.gd _update_sky

  • New uniforms u_space (S, per frame from _update_sky) and space_color (Aero.SPACE_SKY = sRGB (2,3,10) — a near-black with the faintest blue, not a crushed pure black).
  • The atmospheric gradient (incl. horizon haze — one mechanism, no extra knob) is mixed toward space_color with a direction-dependent weight w = mix(S², S, u): the zenith (u=1) fades at S, the horizon (u=0) at S². Physical motivation: the air column above the eye thins first; the grazing horizon path keeps its blue longest. At S=0 the sky is bit-identical to pre-AC-0386 (S²=S=0 — no change anywhere below 500 m); at S=1 the whole dome is space.
  • The sun disc stays (it is the real sun in space); the sun/moon glow (a scattering effect) fades by (1−S). The moon-flip logic (AC-0235) is untouched.

2.3 Stars — core/star.gdshader + main.gd

  • Opacity = (1 − day) + day·S = mix(1−day, 1, S). Below the band: bit-identical to today (night stars at ground level are preserved — AC-0035 web parity, zero ground-behavior change). Above the band: the space sky is black at every time of day, so the field is fully on — the MC-style above-the-sky-limit look (stars in the daytime space sky, exactly where the user wants the "left the planet" moment to land). In between: smooth.
  • Planet occlusion (new, fixes a pre-existing flaw): a star whose direction ray-hits the body disc (sphere centred (0,−R,0), radius R+SEA = the body's own radius, where its rim dissolve starts blending to u_air) is hidden. The camera position is an explicit u_cam_pos uniform (the headless dummy renderer rejects the CAMERA_POSITION builtin — the AC-0036 precedent). This removes the night-orbit artifact where star dots painted on top of the planet. Residual known artifact (documented, sub-pixel): a star whose center just clears the body rim can still paint over the thin (R+SEA → R+400) cloud-ring annulus — the clouds are value-noise alpha, no per-direction coverage test is owed.
  • Source/twinkle: no design decision needed — the field already exists (500 stars, per-star size/hue/brightness, MC-style shapes) and needs no twinkle. The "3-D vs skybox" question resolves itself: it is already a skybox in disguise (camera-position-following, world-fixed orientation, zero parallax) — only its visibility was missing the altitude term.

2.4 AeroWash — fades by (1−S)

The screen wash is an air-in-front-of-the-lens tint; in space there is no air. One uniform per frame (wash_amount = WASH_AMOUNT·(1−S)); the quad stays up (cost is the uniform, not the quad).

2.5 What deliberately did NOT change (the double-darkening answer)

The space gradient is applied to the sky-pass BACKGROUND ONLY — to no geometry, no fog, no u_air. env.fog_light_color, env.background_color and the satellite's u_air all stay DayNight.sky_display(t) at every altitude — the existing fog figures are the reference and are untouched. Why the ground seen from orbit cannot be double-darkened: at altitude the local terrain is faded by depth fog alone (saturating at 714 m, unchanged); every ray toward the planet that reaches that far is the fog color; beyond the render edge the planet disc is the satellite body, which dissolves into the same atmospheric color at its rim; the sky background is only visible above the limb — where there is no ground to darken. Result: a planet with a blue atmospheric limb (fog color + cloud shells R+275..400) hanging against a genuinely black sky — the planet is in its atmosphere, and the sky now says vacuum. Consistent with the body's ladder (rim/dissolve→full by 926 m, before the band completes at 2000 m) and with the AC-0385 cloud shells, which wrap the disc as its weather.

4.3 The horizon at the low and high ends

  • Low end (alt ≤ 500 m): pixel-identical to today — the haze-lifted blue (181, 217, 224) at noon, (6, 33, 48) at night. S = 0 makes the whole AC-0386 path vanish; nothing moves below the band.
  • High end (alt ≥ 2000 m): the sky background in every direction is the space color. But the horizon the player sees from orbit is not the background — it is the planet's limb: the depth-fog wall (714 m, the unchanged reference) + the satellite body's rim dissolve into u_air = DayNight.sky_display(t) + the cloud shells R+275..400 hugging the disc. So the high-end horizon is a blue atmospheric limb against a black sky — the planet in its atmosphere, and the sky now honestly says vacuum. That is the "moment of leaving" landing: at 2000 m (band = 1, the 6-DOF handover) the sky is fully space, stars fully on, and the planet is a blue-rimmed disc below.

4.4 tasks/AC-0386/space-gradient.png

A data-driven strip generated from the same fragment math (no rendering). What a viewer should expect: four panels. (1) "zenith sky, noon" — a vertical strip, bottom = 0 m, top = 8000 m: flat day-blue from the bottom to the 500 m mark, then a smooth darkening through deep navy, reaching black at the 2000 m line and staying black to the top. (2) "horizon sky, noon" — the same layout but the blue persists visibly LONGER (the S² lag: at the 1250 m mark the strip is still mid-blue while panel 1 is already navy), converging to black at 2000 m. (3) "zenith sky, midnight" — already near-black throughout, deepening to black at 2000 m. (4) "space S + star opacity" — the curve: the gold line is S (flat 0 to 500 m, S-curve through the band, 1 from 2000 m), the blue line (noon star opacity) overlaps it exactly, the dashed violet (midnight star opacity) is flat 1.0 across the whole axis. The two flat regions and the single smooth S through the band are the no-pop property, drawn.
