01

Goal & the three stories

Three player-facing outcomes, in strict order — each one is a prerequisite for the next, because "off the planet" and "the planet looks round from space" are meaningless until the ground is a sphere.

#Story (player language)What makes it truePiece
1 Walk on a round planet that plays flat. The horizon dips and distant ground sinks, but mining, placing, stepping and building feel exactly like flat Minecraft — and I can walk to the edge of the region I am in, cross onto the next part of the planet, and keep going. Every live chunk is placed on the sphere by rotation; block size stays 1 m; the flat-grid consumers (collision, rays, camera, spawn, recenter) know about the sphere; a face boundary is crossable in both directions. P1–P4
2 Fly off the planet and land again. Gravity always pulls toward the planet's centre, walking stays tangent-flat, and above the atmosphere I get free 6-DOF flight — then I can land on any face. Up-vector derived from position; movement projected onto the tangent plane; altitude blend from surface gravity to free flight; landing re-orients to the local up. P5
3 See the planet as a sphere from orbit — a satellite view that dissolves into real voxels as I descend, with no pop. A whole-body tier above an altitude threshold, fed by the height-only far data, fading against the existing LOD ladder. P6

The flagship acceptance test follows from the geometry: the planet's circumference is the same as one lap of the flat world's four-face circuit, so "walk around the planet and come back to spawn" is a literal, checkable statement — about 25 km of ground, roughly an hour and a half on foot and about sixteen minutes at cruise flight speed. That is the demo for story 1.

The one invariant everything else is shaped by AC-0042's hard gate still holds and is still the right one: curvature is a placement and camera truth, not a block-size change. If a piece of this epic moves genhash off 25/25, it has changed the terrain instead of the planet, and it is wrong.

05

The grid lock

Two grids have to agree on a cube-sphere: the data grid (integer columns, 1 m apart in flat coordinates) and the surface (arc metres on the sphere). Today they do not, and the mismatch is one wrong ratio — not a wrong radius.

A lap of the flat world crosses four cube faces; the sphere's circumference is 2πR. Holding those equal is exactly the statement "one flat metre is one metre of arc": 

lap of the flat world = 4 · W columns        (4 cube faces, W columns each)
sphere circumference  = 2πR
→ 1 flat metre = 1 m of arc   ⇔   W / R = π/2 = 1.5708

shipped keying:  cx = R·u   →   W = 2R    (ratio 2.000)
1.5708 / 2.000 = 0.785   →   0.72 m of arc per block (0.62–0.79 across the face)

So the fix is a ratio, and R stays 4000: pin W = πR/2 = 6283 columns per cube face (3141 per half). The shipped 8000 is simply the keying tying the face's width to R — that tie is the defect. The same rule covers any planet: W = 1.5708·R, so a bigger planet takes more columns per face. That scaling is why no single fixed grid can give 1 m everywhere — a sphere is not developable.

Four concrete changes, and one thing that deliberately does not change:

  1. Pin the ratio. W/R = π/2 (= 1.5708), so W = 6283 columns per cube face at R = 4000; per planet, W = 1.5708·R.
  2. Hold R = 4000. Game.planet_R, the planets[] save record, the [2000,8000] clamp, the horizon (114 m), the chunk tilt (0.23°) and the 384 m tower sightline (~1753 m) are all untouched — there is no save migration for the radius.
  3. Map by arc. uv_to_world / world_to_face / key_for_sphere_pos's inverse become arc-based (u = x/W), with a 2D pre-warp so spacing is even across the face.
  4. Assert it. The sphere arm gains the spacing check P1a never had: the on-sphere distance between adjacent columns, sampled at several positions. P1a asserted gaplessness only.
What does not change: the storage keys On the home pair a column is its flat x/z, and it stays that way — the identity survives, so genhash, the save key format and every arm that reads the flat world are untouched. Only the position → cell ratio and direction change.

Two honest caveats. First, a cube-sphere is not isometric: after pre-warping, spacing is 1.00 m along the face midlines, ~0.92 m averaged over a face, and ~0.85 m toward the corners. AC-0042's "2–5%" was optimistic; this spread is the irreducible part, and it is smallest where the player plays. Second, the accepted consequence of holding R: the home pair's flat patch is now 6283 × 6283 m, so a cube edge is 3141 m from spawn rather than 4000 m. The planet is the same size — the flat net simply wraps sooner — and that is precisely why cross-face movement is a first-class piece.

06

Placement and the look

6.1 How a chunk sits on the sphere

With the grid locked (D2), placement is a rotation. Each 16×H×16 column is a rigid flat box whose node transform is chosen so that its local +Y points along the radial at its own centre, and neighbouring chunks are rotated about their shared edge so they meet exactly — the folded-net property AC-0042 illustrated: a folded cube is gapless where a flat net is detached.

PropertyRigid facets (chosen)Per-vertex warp (rejected for the ground)
SurfacePolyhedron of 16 m facets; adjacent facets differ by 0.23° at R = 4000Smooth sphere; blocks become very slightly trapezoidal
Local spaceUnchanged — meshing, greedy thresholds, light grid, collision all still operate on flat local coordinatesMesh emit path changes (C++ AweMesh); visuals and rigid collision no longer coincide exactly unless mirrored
Gatesgenhash 25/25 by constructionNeeds the warp mirrored anywhere geometry is assumed flat
Where it is usedThe whole playable surfaceOnly the satellite tier (§6.3), where a true sphere is free

One consequence exists in every option and should not be mistaken for a placement bug: columns diverge with height. Blocks are 1 m cubes stacked along each chunk's own normal, so at +384 m the gap between adjacent columns is H/R per block — 9.6 cm per column at +384 m, R = 4000. Digging shows the mirror: a 7.5 cm overlap, hidden because interior faces are culled. This is exactly what AC-0042's hidden filler ("grout") and the corner seal are for.

6.2 The horizon is the whole "looks round" promise on the ground

At R = 4000 the ground horizon from eye height 1.62 m is 114 m; a 384 m tower's top is visible out to ~1753 m. Curvature is visible in normal play without any new rendering feature — provided the fog and the LOW tier are tuned to not hide it, which is one of the reasons the lighting review's real-disc question (AC-0299) matters to this epic.

6.3 The satellite tier (story 3)

Above an altitude threshold, the whole body is drawn as one object and the per-chunk ladder fades out. It is a new tier, and deliberately built from data that already exists rather than from a fresh noise bake:

AspectDesign
Source dataThe far column payload the pipeline already produces: [H u16×256][biome×256][top×256] per chunk — height, biome and top block are everything a body texture needs (colour from the palette, shading from H).
BakePer-face texture at satellite resolution (1024² per face = 12.6 M samples for the planet). At the standing far cost of ~92 µs per 256-column chunk that is roughly 4.5 s of CPU, one-time per seed, at low priority; a full-resolution bake would be ~138 s and is not needed for a body view. Estimate from standing values — the ticket measures it.
MeshA true sphere (this is where per-vertex smoothness belongs). The limb is the sphere itself, so the silhouette is right by construction.
Threshold & crossfadeDefined against the real ladder, not the old plan's 2000/500 m: the sim disc is 4 chunks (64 m) and real geometry ends at render_dist 50 chunks (800 m). The crossfade must also fade the halo/far tier or the body double-draws. Note the band immediately beyond the sim radius is itself being changed: AC-0299 (b), answered 2026-09-17, makes it draw HIGH-LOD textured chunks out to the medium start (cave-free slabs materialised from H + biome + top), which raises the ladder the satellite tier sits above.
Inner edgeThe render distance — where the drawn world currently stops (settings.gd:45-46: "NOTHING renders past the render distance, data-only ahead of it"; world/world.gd:1698 tier 3 is data-only). Derive it from the setting, never hard-code 800 m, and survive a runtime change. The fade is per-fragment by view distance so there is no ring at the edge, and the real disc fades out entirely at altitude instead of sitting as a bright patch inside the body. This edge is also what lets the curved horizon be seen: at eye height the horizon is 114 m, but from a 384 m tower the sightline is ~1753 m — well past the 800 m disc.
ReuseBuild it as a planet body keyed by (planet_id, R, seed), not a one-off for the home world — that is the same object the solar-system work needs for other planets, so it pays twice.
AtmosphereReuse the existing sky, cloud, aero and day/night shaders for the haze that hides the dissolve; do not invent a second atmosphere.
07

Cross-face movement

"Move between faces" is not one problem. Crossing a boundary touches six separate contracts, and each one has a different cost — which is why it gets its own piece instead of being assumed to work once placement lands.

#ContractTodayApproach
C1Terrain — the far side must be ground, not a cliff into voidFaces 2–11 are independent salted flat patches; the code records "face-border terrain seams acceptable"Interim: a blend band near the boundary (a few tens of columns where the face field fades into a position-based field). End state: sphere-domain generation (P7), after which the band is deleted.
C2Grid — the two sides must be comparable2:1 cell ratio across cube edges; the 1024-cell stub grid on faces 2–11Solved by the grid lock (§05): same cell size on both sides, so a crossing is a key remap via SphereMath.neighbor_key — already gated.
C3Meshing — no coplanarity assumptionsPer-chunk greedy; no cross-face mergeKeep it: greedy stays per-chunk, which is a cosmetic seam at worst. Only the "no cross-face merge" rule needs stating.
C4Lighting — no light seam at the boundaryAweStarlight propagates inside the sim band; the halo covers beyondSeed the neighbouring face's chunk into the engine when a boundary enters the sim band — the halo promote/demote path (AC-0283 P3) and the promotion contract (AC-0286) are the precedent to reuse, not a new mechanism.
C5Collision & routing — no fall-through, camera and spawn stay honestPer-chunk StaticBody3D; the voxel DDA and camera assume Y-up and a flat (cx,cz) gridPhysics bodies placed by rotation and tiled across the boundary; position → face → chunk routing continuous; camera up from the radial. Shares its work with the consumers piece.
C6Save — edits on the far side persistAlready keyed planet:face:ccx:ccz:localNothing to build. Worth an explicit round-trip assertion near a boundary so it stays true.
A useful accident of the 12-face split The home pair already contains a boundary — the x=0 midline — and it is 1:1 by construction and covered by the existing arm. So the first cross-face test does not need any travel at all: it is in the middle of the home world. The expensive boundaries (x, z = ±3141) are the ones that need C1's terrain policy.

#RiskMitigation
R1The seam is worse than the plan thinks. The shipped topology has a 2:1 cell ratio and independent salted patches, and the plan's "walk straight across" was never implemented.The grid lock removes the ratio; C1's blend band is the interim; P7 removes the need for a band. R3Double-draw at the satellite threshold. The halo/far tier and the body could both draw the same ground.The crossfade consumes the tiers explicitly, and the gate looks for it rather than trusting the fade.
R4P7's re-baseline invalidates terrain evidence accumulated by the earlier pieces (genhash baseline, gen ms/col, boundary r4, the cross-face continuity proof, renders), and it deletes the band they were proven with.Accepted deliberately: P7 is last by decision, the blend band is the interim, and the re-baseline is one gate run plus a doc update — the same cost the cave series pays four times. The pieces re-prove boundary continuity after P7 rather than trusting the earlier evidence.
R5The flat patch per face shrinks. R6The whole planet is mistaken for a persistent world. A planet at 1 m resolution is hundreds of millions of columns.Only the sim band plus edits persist (AC-0287 makes far columns ephemeral by design). Nothing in this epic tries to save a planet.
R7Plan-vs-code drift recurs — this document ages the same way AC-0042 did.Each ticket owes its own ARCHITECTURE.md sync (standing invariant 2), and the §03 drift check is repeated at the epic's closeout rather than trusted.
R8Old worlds stop loading as the pieces change the world's shape and the far/sim save filter lands.Accepted by policy, not mitigated: old worlds are disposable during development (user, 2026-09-17 — godot/ARCHITECTURE.md §5). No migration is owed; a format change still bumps SAVE_VERSION so an old world is rejected cleanly and starts fresh instead of half-loading. Migration is a deliberate choice, never a default.
